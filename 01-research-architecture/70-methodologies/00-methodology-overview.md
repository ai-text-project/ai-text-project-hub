# Structural Representation Methodology

**Methodology Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

Structural Representation Methodology defines the reusable operational methodology that has emerged across independently completed Structural Representation repositories.

The objective of this methodology is not to describe any individual theoretical system.

Instead, it externalizes the common operational principles repeatedly observed during the construction of multiple Structural Representation repositories.

The methodology therefore functions as reusable research infrastructure within the Research Architecture.

---

# Position within the Research Architecture

Structural Representation Methodology occupies the Methodology Layer of the Research Architecture.

Its responsibility begins after individual Structural Representation repositories have been completed.

Accordingly, the methodology is externalized through observation of completed repositories rather than designed prior to implementation.

The relationship is therefore represented as follows.

Completed Structural Representation Repositories

↓

Methodological Emergence Assessment

↓

Structural Representation Methodology

↓

Future Structural Representation Projects

---

# Methodological Scope

The methodology documents reusable operational principles that repeatedly appear across completed Structural Representation repositories.

Its scope includes:

- Representation Pipeline
- Structural Assets
- Validation Methodology
- Repository Organization Principles
- Architectural Boundaries

The methodology intentionally excludes theory-specific implementation.

---

# Fundamental Principle

The methodology follows the Observation First principle adopted throughout the Research Program.

Methodological knowledge is externalized only after common operational patterns have been observed across independently completed repositories.

Accordingly, the methodology records historical methodological emergence rather than prescribing implementation procedures.

---

# Relationship to Case Studies

Individual Structural Representation repositories remain independent case studies.

They demonstrate practical implementation of the methodology but do not define the methodology itself.

The relationship is therefore represented as follows.

Methodology

↓

Independent Case Studies

- ACIM Structural Representation
- World Model Structural Representation
- AI Textbook Structural Representation
- Future Structural Representation Projects

Each repository preserves its own theoretical domain while sharing common methodological organization.

---

# Architectural Components

Structural Representation Methodology consists of multiple reusable methodological components.

These include:

- Problem Definition
- Representation Pipeline
- Structural Assets
- Validation Methodology

Additional methodological components may be externalized as further completed repositories become available.

---

# Architectural Boundary

Structural Representation Methodology is not responsible for:

- Constructing Structural Representations
- Evaluating theoretical correctness
- Comparing conceptual systems
- Redesigning completed repositories
- Producing educational explanations

These responsibilities remain assigned to other research roles within the Research Program.

---

# Evolution Policy

Structural Representation Methodology is expected to evolve through repeated observation of newly completed Structural Representation repositories.

Future revisions should reflect additional methodological evidence rather than redesign existing methodological principles.

---

# Summary

Structural Representation Methodology provides the reusable methodological layer connecting independently completed Structural Representation repositories.

Its purpose is to preserve reusable operational knowledge while maintaining the independence of individual case studies.

The methodology therefore serves as stable research infrastructure supporting future Structural Representation projects across multiple conceptual domains.
