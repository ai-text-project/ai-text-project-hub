**Repository:** ACIM Structural Representation Repository

**Version:** v0.1 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the primary dependency relationships among the Functional Modules identified within the ACIM Structural Representation.

Unlike the Module Map, which identifies individual organizational units, the Dependency Map specifies how these modules depend upon one another to form a coherent structural architecture.

The objective is not to describe conceptual meaning.

The objective is not to define operational pathways.

Instead, this document externalizes the organizational dependencies that stabilize the overall structural architecture.

# Dependency Principle

A dependency relationship represents an architectural requirement rather than a temporal sequence.

The Dependency Map therefore describes structural necessity instead of process order.

Dependencies answer the question:

"What organizational components must already exist before another component can function?"

---

# Primary Dependency Structure

## Dependency Level 1

Foundational Architecture

↓

Structural Generation

Structural Generation depends upon the existence of the foundational layer.

Without the ontological foundation, structural differentiation cannot occur.

---

## Dependency Level 2

Structural Generation

↓

Development Architecture

Development Architecture depends upon previously generated structural differentiation.

Without structural generation, developmental organization cannot emerge.

---

## Dependency Level 3

Development Architecture

↓

Recovery Architecture

Recovery Architecture assumes the existence of a developed structural organization.

Recovery therefore depends upon developmental organization.

---

## Dependency Level 4

Recovery Architecture

↓

Operational Architecture

Operational Architecture externalizes structural recovery into continuous practical operation.

Operational processes therefore depend upon Recovery Architecture.

# Cross-Layer Dependencies

Certain Functional Modules participate across multiple organizational layers.

Examples include:

- Decision Module
- Guidance Module
- Vision Module
- Learning Module

These modules are structurally reusable.

They participate in multiple dependency relationships without altering the stability of the primary organizational hierarchy.

# Dependency Characteristics

The Dependency Map identifies three forms of dependency.

## Hierarchical Dependency

Vertical organizational dependence.

Example

Foundation

↓

Generation

---

## Functional Dependency

Dependence created by structural responsibility.

Example

Guidance Module

↓

Vision Module

---

## Organizational Dependency

Dependence created by participation in the overall architecture.

Example

Operational Architecture depends upon Recovery Architecture.

---

# Boundary

The responsibility of the Dependency Map ends after the primary architectural dependencies have been externalized.

The following responsibilities belong to subsequent structural assets.

Architecture Matrix

- organizational responsibility assignment

Concept Network

- conceptual connectivity

Structural Topology

- complete structural network

Canonical Structural Representation

- reusable architectural representation

Accordingly, the Dependency Map intentionally excludes:

- communication pathways;
- operational topology;
- structural loops;
- comparative observation.

---

# Completion Criterion

The Dependency Map is considered complete when every Functional Module possesses an identifiable structural dependency within the organizational architecture.

The resulting Dependency Map should explain why the architecture remains structurally coherent independently of the original ACIM resource.

---

# Design Freeze

Once approved, the dependency hierarchy should remain stable.

Future observations may refine local dependencies.

However, modifications to the primary dependency hierarchy should occur only when a fundamental architectural inconsistency has been identified.
