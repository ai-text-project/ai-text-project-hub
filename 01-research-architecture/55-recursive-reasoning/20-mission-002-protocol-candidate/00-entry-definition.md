# Purpose

This document defines the entry conditions, research question, scope boundary, and developmental constraints for Mission 002 — Protocol Candidate Formation.

Mission 001 established that a recurring recursive reasoning structure exists across multiple Research Program roles and reasoning contexts.

Its principal stabilized output was:

> **Recursive Reasoning Pattern v0.2 — Evidence-Supported**

Mission 002 begins from that result.

Its responsibility is not to rediscover the pattern.

Its responsibility is not to assume that the pattern is already a protocol.

Instead, Mission 002 asks whether the observed pattern can be intentionally operationalized without exceeding the evidence from which it emerged.

The governing transition is:

**Observed Pattern**

↓

**Operationalization Analysis**

↓

**Protocol Candidate**

not:

**Observed Pattern**

↓

**Validated Protocol**

# Primary Research Question

The primary Mission 002 research question is:

> **Can Recursive Reasoning Pattern v0.2 be intentionally operationalized into a reusable Protocol Candidate while preserving the evidence-supported behavior observed in Mission 001?**

This question contains two requirements.

## Operational Reproducibility

Can the observed behavior be expressed in a form that can intentionally guide future reasoning?

## Evidential Fidelity

Can this be done without introducing unsupported mechanisms or converting descriptive observations into unjustified prescriptions?

Both conditions are required.

---

# Secondary Research Questions

Mission 002 may examine the following questions.

## Trigger Recognition

Can operational conditions for recursive re-entry be identified?

## Re-entry Decision

Can the reasoning process distinguish when previous-state re-entry is necessary from when current reasoning should simply continue?

## Recursive Target Selection

Can the relevant previous reasoning state or process be identified without globally reopening the research environment?

## Recursive Depth

Can sufficient recursive depth be operationally approximated?

## Preservation

Can unaffected stable structure be protected during recursive reconsideration?

## Preserve / Revise Decision

Can re-evaluation remain genuinely open to both preservation and revision?

## Restabilization

Can operational stopping or restabilization conditions be identified?

## Human–AI Coupling

How should Human and AI contributions be represented without assuming that one participant exclusively controls recursive reasoning?

These are operationalization questions.

They are not yet protocol rules.

Mission 002 is the first stage in this research sequence where limited intentional design becomes legitimate.

The developmental order is:

# Pattern Preservation Requirement

Protocol formation must preserve the following evidence-supported characteristics unless later validation provides a reason to revise them.

## Conditional Re-entry

Recursive re-entry is not mandatory.

## Selective Target

Only relevant previous states should be reopened.

## Selective Recursive Depth

Recursive depth should be sufficient rather than maximal.

## Genuine Re-evaluation

The previous state must remain genuinely revisable.

## Preserve / Revise Symmetry

Preservation and revision must both remain legitimate outcomes.

## Stable Structure Preservation

Unaffected stable structure should remain preserved where possible.

## Restabilization

Recursive reasoning should not become an indefinite loop.

Any Protocol Candidate that systematically violates these characteristics should be treated as inconsistent with the Mission 001 evidence.

# Repository Role

Mission 001 established that repository assets can function as operational reasoning resources.

Mission 002 may therefore consider repository interaction as part of protocol operationalization.

However:

> **Repository access is an enabling mechanism, not the definition of recursive reasoning.**

The recursive object may include:

- repository documents;
- externalized reasoning states;
- previous interpretations;
- validation states;
- reasoning trajectories;
- previously stabilized processes.

Protocol formation must therefore avoid reducing recursive reasoning to file retrieval.

---

# Human–AI Boundary

Mission 001 found Human–AI Coupling to be an evidence-supported contextual feature.

Mission 002 may examine how recursive responsibilities are distributed across Human and AI participants.

However, it must not assume in advance that:

- the Human always supplies the trigger;
- the AI always performs re-evaluation;
- either participant has exclusive control;
- the observed coupled process automatically transfers to autonomous AI reasoning.

The operational relationship must remain an object of analysis.

---

Mission 002 may investigate such a mechanism.

> **Protocol Candidate Mechanism**

> **Validated Pattern Feature**

# Selective Recursive Depth Boundary

Selective Recursive Depth is one of the strongest evidence-supported additions in Pattern v0.2.

Mission 002 must therefore preserve the distinction between:

**Insufficient Re-entry**

and:

**Excessive Re-entry**

The operational objective is not:

> **Maximum recursion**

but potentially:

> **Sufficient recursion for the current reasoning requirement**

Mission 002 must determine whether this principle can be made operational without pretending that an optimal recursive depth is already known.

- Reflection-IN / Reflection-ON;

# Protocol Candidate Standard

A Mission 002 Protocol Candidate should satisfy at least four conditions.

## Traceability

Each major protocol component should be traceable to Pattern v0.2 or explicitly identified as a designed mechanism.

## Minimality

The protocol should introduce no more mechanism than necessary to operationalize the observed pattern.

## Revisability

The protocol must remain open to validation-driven revision.

## Testability

The protocol should be sufficiently explicit that later operational cases can determine whether it functions as intended.

A protocol that cannot be distinguished from general advice is not sufficiently operationalized.

# Success Condition

Mission 002 will be successful if it can determine whether Pattern v0.2 can be converted into a coherent and testable Protocol Candidate while maintaining explicit traceability to Mission 001 evidence.

Success does not require the Protocol Candidate to be validated.

**Observed Pattern**

**Operational Interpretation**

**Designed Mechanism**

# Stop Condition

Mission 002 should stop when:

- the operational requirements of Pattern v0.2 are sufficiently understood;
- necessary designed mechanisms are explicitly identified;
- unsupported assumptions are separated from evidence-supported features;
- a coherent Protocol Candidate can be stated;
- validation requirements for that candidate are identifiable.

At that point, further work should move from:

**Protocol Candidate Formation**

to:

**Protocol Validation**

rather than continuing to elaborate the candidate indefinitely.

---

# Failure Conditions

Mission 002 should not proceed toward Protocol Candidate status if operationalization requires:

- contradicting core Pattern v0.2 evidence;
- mandatory recursion regardless of trigger;
- global restart as the default re-entry strategy;
- maximum recursive depth as a general rule;
- automatic revision of previous states;
- unsupported assumptions about Human–AI control;
- mechanisms that cannot be distinguished from theoretical speculation.

If such conditions arise, Mission 002 should return to operationalization analysis rather than force protocol formation.
