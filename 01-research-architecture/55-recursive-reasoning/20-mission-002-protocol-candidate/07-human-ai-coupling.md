# Purpose

This document externalizes the current operational understanding of:

> **Human–AI Coupling within Recursive Reasoning**

developed during Mission 002.

The purpose is not to construct a general theory of Human–AI collaboration.

The purpose is not to prescribe a universal Human-in-the-loop architecture.

The purpose is not to determine whether Human or AI should possess final authority in every reasoning environment.

The purpose is narrower.

It is to identify:

> **what operational responsibilities must be represented when Human and AI both contribute to, influence, observe, constrain, or permit the continuation of a Recursive Reasoning process.**

The document therefore focuses on the structural relationship between:

```text
Human Participation
        +
AI Participation
        +
Current Reasoning State
        +
Current Reasoning Process
        +
Authority / Operational Latitude
```

within the Recursive Reasoning mechanism developed across Mission 002.

# Observed Human–AI Interaction Pattern

A simple instruction-response model does not adequately describe the observed process.

The simplistic pattern would be:

```text
Human
        ↓
Instruction
        ↓
AI
        ↓
Output
        ↓
Human
```

But Mission 002 repeatedly exhibited something closer to:

```text
AI Reasoning
        ↓
Human Observation
        ↓
Reasoning State changes
        ↓
AI Re-examination
        ↓
AI Candidate Formation
        ↓
Human Judgment /
Intervention /
Non-intervention
        ↓
Reasoning continues
        ↓
Counter-test
        ↓
Human or AI detects
new material issue
        ↓
Further reasoning
```

This interaction pattern is neither:

```text
pure Human control
```

nor:

```text
pure AI autonomy
```

It is better understood initially as:

> **a coupled reasoning process with distinguishable participant contributions and boundaries.**

# Why Simple Turn-Taking Is Insufficient

Human–AI Coupling cannot be reduced to conversational turn order.

For example:

```text
Human speaks
        ↓
AI speaks
        ↓
Human speaks
```

describes communication sequence.

It does not tell us:

- what entered the Reasoning State
- what changed the Reasoning Process
- whether a statement had decision authority
- whether a recommendation was binding
- whether silence preserved existing latitude
- whether intervention changed scope
- whether a participant merely contributed evidence
- whether the participant changed operational direction

Therefore:

> **Turn-taking is not an adequate unit of analysis for Human–AI Coupling.**

# Reasoning State as Shared Operational Object

A stronger candidate is to treat:

> **the current Reasoning State**

as the primary operational object.

Human and AI may both affect this object.

A provisional representation is:

```text
Human
  │
  │ contribution
  ▼
Current Reasoning State
  ▲
  │ contribution
  │
 AI
```

However, this is still incomplete.

Not every interaction merely adds content to the Reasoning State.

Some interactions change:

> **how reasoning is allowed or expected to proceed.**

Therefore Reasoning-State Contribution alone is insufficient.

# Initial Coupling Observations

The early examination identified four recurring interaction forms:

```text
A. Observation

B. Judgment

C. Intervention

D. Non-intervention
```

These were initially treated as candidate Coupling Modes.

They are not yet assumed to be independent mechanisms.

---

# A — Observation

A Human may observe something about the reasoning process without explicitly instructing the AI to perform a particular operation.

For example, a Human may notice:

> **different MD targets appear to be examined through similar reasoning procedures.**

Operationally:

```text
Human Observation
        ↓
Potentially Material Information
        ↓
Current Reasoning State
        ↓
AI evaluates relevance
```

This can change subsequent reasoning even though the Human did not issue a direct command.

---

# Observation Is Not Instruction

Therefore:

```text
Observation
        ≠
Instruction
```

A Human may contribute information without specifying what must happen next.

Likewise, an AI may surface an observation without possessing authority to determine the next operation.

Thus:

> **Reasoning Contribution must be distinguishable from Process Control.**

---

# Observation Is Not Automatically Material

Human-originated information must not automatically be treated as materially decisive.

Likewise, AI-originated information must not automatically be treated as materially decisive.

Therefore:

```text
Human Observation
        ↓
Materiality Examination
```

and:

```text
AI Observation
        ↓
Materiality Examination
```

remain compatible with the broader Recursive Reasoning process.

The source of an observation does not by itself determine its structural effect.

---

# B — Judgment

A Human may express a judgment such as:

```text
This direction is acceptable.
```

or:

```text
Freeze is appropriate.
```

or:

```text
This appears sufficiently stable.
```

An AI may similarly generate a judgment such as:

```text
Externalization Ready.
```

or:

```text
Further exploration is not justified.
```

Judgment therefore appears to differ from simple Observation.

---

# Judgment Is Not Structural Truth

However:

```text
Human Judgment
        ≠
Structural Truth
```

and:

```text
AI Judgment
        ≠
Structural Truth
```

For example:

```text
Human:
"This appears Restabilized."
```

does not automatically establish:

```text
RESTABILIZED
```

if material incoherence remains.

Likewise:

```text
AI:
"Restabilized."
```

does not make the reasoning state structurally stable merely because the statement was generated.

Therefore:

> **Participant Judgment and Structural Condition must remain distinguishable.**

---

# Judgment May Affect Reasoning

Although Judgment is not Structural Truth, it can materially affect the Reasoning Process.

For example:

```text
Human Judgment
        ↓
"Proceed"
        ↓
Current Direction Maintained
```

or:

```text
Human Judgment
        ↓
"Hold"
        ↓
Current Branch Suspended
```

Thus Judgment may operate simultaneously as:

```text
Reasoning-State Contribution
+
Reasoning-Process Influence
```

depending on context.

---

# C — Intervention

Human intervention occurs when Human participation materially changes the way reasoning proceeds.

Examples include:

```text
Do not create another branch.

Hold this observation.

Use Accelerated Mode.

Stop exploration here.

Externalize this result.

Do not require Independent Validation now.
```

Operationally:

```text
Current Reasoning Process
        ↓
Human Intervention
        ↓
Reasoning Latitude /
Direction /
Constraint changes
```

This is stronger than merely contributing descriptive information.

---

# Intervention Is Not Necessarily Micromanagement

Human intervention does not imply that the Human must control every subsequent reasoning step.

For example:

```text
Human:
"Do not create additional branches."
        ↓
Constraint established
        ↓
AI performs multiple
bounded reasoning operations
within that constraint
```

Therefore:

```text
Human Intervention
        ≠
Human Micromanagement
```

This distinction later becomes important for Operational Latitude.

---

# D — Non-intervention

One of the most informative observations during Mission 002 was explicit Human non-intervention.

This appeared operationally as:

> **静観モード / Observation Mode**

During this state, the Human intentionally refrained from adding unnecessary branches or micro-instructions while the AI continued bounded examination.

A simplified representation is:

```text
Human
        ↓
Explicit Non-intervention
        ↓
Existing Reasoning Direction
and Constraints remain active
        ↓
AI continues bounded
Operational Examination
```

---

# Non-intervention Is Not Nothing

It would be incorrect to represent this simply as:

```text
Human Input = 0
```

because explicit Non-intervention occurred within an already established reasoning context.

The Human remained able to:

- observe
- interrupt
- redirect
- object
- approve
- reject
- request clarification
- change constraints

Therefore:

> **Non-intervention occurred within retained participation capacity.**

---

# Non-intervention Is Not Human Absence

Thus:

```text
Non-intervention
        ≠
Human Absence
```

The Human participant remained part of the operational environment even while not actively directing each reasoning step.

---

# Non-intervention Is Not Consent

Similarly:

```text
Human Silence
        ≠
Consent
```

A Human who does not intervene at a particular moment has not necessarily approved every inference or future action.

This is especially important if the protocol is later generalized beyond the current research context.

---

# Non-intervention Is Not Authority Transfer

Likewise:

```text
Non-intervention
        ≠
Authority Transfer
```

The absence of active intervention does not itself establish that authority has been transferred to AI.

---

# Non-intervention Is Not Unlimited Delegation

Nor does it imply:

```text
AI may now do anything.
```

Instead, the observed pattern is closer to:

```text
Existing Objective
        +
Existing Constraints
        +
Existing Reasoning Direction
        +
Human Non-intervention
        ↓
AI continues within
bounded Operational Latitude
```

---

# Initial Four-Mode Candidate

The first descriptive candidate can therefore be represented as:

```text
Human Participation
│
├── Observation
│
├── Judgment
│
├── Intervention
│
└── Non-intervention
```

At first glance these appear to be four distinct Coupling Modes.

However, creating four independent mechanisms would increase conceptual complexity.

Therefore they must be tested for compression.

---

# Why Compression Is Necessary

Mission 002 has repeatedly followed the principle:

> **Do not preserve distinctions merely because they can be named.**

A distinction should remain operationally separate only when collapsing it would destroy necessary reasoning responsibility.

Therefore the relevant question is not:

> Can Observation, Judgment, Intervention, and Non-intervention be described separately?

They clearly can.

The relevant question is:

> **Must they be separate Core Mechanisms?**

---

# Compression Test — Observation and Judgment

Observation and Judgment differ semantically.

Observation may look like:

```text
"The reasoning procedure
appears similar across MDs."
```

Judgment may look like:

```text
"This direction is acceptable."
```

But both may enter the reasoning process as:

```text
Human
        ↓
Meaningful Information
        ↓
Current Reasoning State
```

Therefore both can initially be grouped under:

> **Human-originated Reasoning-State Contribution**

without erasing their semantic differences.

---

# Semantic Difference vs Mechanism Difference

This yields an important distinction:

```text
Different Input Semantics
        ≠
Different Core Mechanism
```

Observation and Judgment may require different interpretation.

But they need not necessarily require separate Coupling architectures.

Thus:

> **Input semantics should be preserved without automatically multiplying mechanisms.**

---

# Compression Test — Intervention and Non-intervention

Intervention appears to change reasoning progression directly.

```text
Intervention
        ↓
Reasoning Process changes
```

Non-intervention appears different because it does not actively redirect the process.

However, explicit Non-intervention may preserve the current operational configuration.

```text
Non-intervention
        ↓
Existing Reasoning Latitude
remains in force
```

Therefore both relate to:

> **the state of the Reasoning Process**

rather than only to informational content.

---

# Process Influence Candidate

This supports a second candidate dimension:

> **Reasoning-Process Influence**

where:

```text
Intervention
        ↓
Process state changes
```

while:

```text
Non-intervention
        ↓
Current bounded process state
is maintained
```

Thus active change and deliberate maintenance can be represented within the same broader operational dimension.

---

# First Compression

The initial four-mode candidate can therefore be compressed provisionally as:

```text
Observation ─────┐
                 │
Judgment ────────┤
                 ▼
       Reasoning-State
         Contribution


Intervention ────┐
                 │
Non-intervention ┤
                 ▼
       Reasoning-Process
          Influence
```

This is a significant reduction.

Instead of four independent Core Mechanisms:

```text
4 Modes
```

the current evidence supports:

```text
2 Operational Dimensions
```

at this stage.

---

# First Operational Dimension

## Reasoning-State Contribution

Working definition:

> **Reasoning-State Contribution is the introduction of information, observation, judgment, evidence, interpretation, candidate structure, objection, or other materially evaluable content into the current Reasoning State by a Human or AI participant.**

The definition is intentionally participant-neutral.

---

# Human Reasoning-State Contribution

Human contributions may include:

```text
Observation
Judgment
Question
Objection
Constraint Information
New Evidence
Context
Preference
Approval
Rejection
Uncertainty
Meta-Observation
```

These do not all have identical semantics.

But they can all become inputs to the current Reasoning State.

---

# AI Reasoning-State Contribution

AI contributions may include:

```text
Observation
Candidate Mechanism
Counter-evidence
Counter-test Result
Structural Distinction
Compression Proposal
Uncertainty
Recommendation
Restabilization Judgment
Placement Judgment
Externalization Proposal
```

Again, these do not have identical semantics.

But they can all become inputs to the current Reasoning State.

---

# Participant-Neutral Contribution Principle

Therefore:

```text
Human Contribution
        ↓
Reasoning State

AI Contribution
        ↓
Reasoning State
```

can share one general operational category.

This does not imply:

```text
Human Evidence
=
AI Evidence
```

or:

```text
Human Authority
=
AI Authority
```

It means only:

> **both participants may contribute materially evaluable content to the Reasoning State.**

---

# Contribution Does Not Determine Materiality

A contribution may be:

```text
Material
```

or:

```text
Non-material
```

or:

```text
Uncertain
```

Therefore:

```text
Contribution
        ≠
Automatic Structural Effect
```

The broader Recursive Reasoning process must still determine what consequence the contribution has.

---

# Contribution Does Not Determine Authority

Likewise:

```text
Contribution
        ≠
Authority
```

A participant may contribute a highly relevant observation without possessing decision authority over the next operation.

Conversely, a participant may possess operational authority without having contributed the decisive evidence.

These must remain separate.

---

# Second Operational Dimension

## Reasoning-Process Influence

Working definition:

> **Reasoning-Process Influence is any Human or AI participation that materially changes, constrains, maintains, redirects, suspends, accelerates, terminates, or proposes alteration to how the current reasoning process proceeds.**

This dimension concerns:

```text
HOW REASONING PROCEEDS
```

rather than only:

```text
WHAT INFORMATION IS PRESENT
```

---

# Human Reasoning-Process Influence

Human Process Influence may include:

```text
Continue.

Stop.

Hold this.

Do not create another branch.

Use Accelerated Mode.

Externalize now.

Do not Externalize yet.

Return to this issue.

Skip this optional validation.

Remain within the current scope.
```

These statements alter or preserve the operational progression of reasoning.

---

# AI Reasoning-Process Influence

AI Process Influence may include:

```text
Recommend continued examination.

Recommend stopping exploration.

Propose Counter-test.

Propose Hold.

Recommend Externalization.

Recommend Freeze.

Request clarification.

Identify need for re-entry.

Propose transition to next MD.
```

These may influence the Reasoning Process even when they are not binding decisions.

---

# Influence Is Not Authority

This produces a critical distinction:

```text
Reasoning-Process Influence
        ≠
Decision Authority
```

A participant may influence the process without possessing final authority over it.

For example:

```text
AI:
"Freeze is recommended."
```

may strongly influence progression.

But:

```text
Recommendation
        ≠
Binding Decision
```

---

# Influence Is Not Structural Truth

Likewise:

```text
Process Influence
        ≠
Epistemic Correctness
```

A Human may redirect reasoning incorrectly.

An AI may recommend stopping prematurely.

Therefore:

> **Process influence describes operational effect, not truth status.**

---

# Contribution and Influence Can Co-occur

A single interaction may instantiate both dimensions.

For example:

```text
Human:
"This dependency appears unresolved.
Please examine it before Freeze."
```

contains:

```text
Reasoning-State Contribution
        +
Reasoning-Process Influence
```

Similarly:

```text
AI:
"A material contradiction remains.
I recommend another Counter-test."
```

contains both.

Therefore:

```text
Contribution
and
Influence
```

are dimensions, not mutually exclusive message categories.

---

# Contribution Without Strong Process Influence

A Human may say:

```text
"This pattern is interesting."
```

This may enter the Reasoning State.

But it may not materially change the current process.

Thus:

```text
Contribution = YES

Process Influence = LOW / NONE
```

is possible.

---

# Process Influence with Minimal New Contribution

Explicit Non-intervention can produce the opposite pattern.

For example:

```text
"静観モード継続です。"
```

may add little new substantive reasoning content.

But it preserves the current operational arrangement.

Thus:

```text
New Reasoning Contribution
        =
Minimal

Process State
        =
Maintained
```

This further supports the independence of the two dimensions.

---

# Two-Dimension Candidate

The emerging structure is therefore:

```text
             HUMAN
               │
       ┌───────┴───────┐
       │               │
       ▼               ▼
Reasoning-State    Reasoning-Process
 Contribution        Influence
       │               │
       └───────┬───────┘
               ▼
        CURRENT REASONING
        STATE / PROCESS
               ▲
       ┌───────┴───────┐
       │               │
       ▼               ▼
Reasoning-State    Reasoning-Process
 Contribution        Influence
       │               │
       └───────AI──────┘
```

This structure is substantially smaller than the original four-mode candidate.

---

# But the Two-Dimension Model Is Incomplete

The two dimensions explain:

```text
WHAT enters reasoning
```

and:

```text
HOW reasoning is influenced
```

But they do not yet answer:

> **Who may actually determine, constrain, continue, or execute what?**

This becomes visible when Human and AI Process Influence are compared.

---

# AI Recommendation Example

Suppose AI generates:

```text
"Exploration should stop here."
```

This is clearly:

```text
Reasoning-Process Influence
```

But whether exploration actually stops may depend on the current operational arrangement.

Therefore:

```text
AI Influence
        ≠
Automatic Authority
```

---

# Human Direction Example

Suppose Human says:

```text
"Stop exploration and Externalize."
```

Within the current Mission 002 interaction, this has a stronger operational effect.

But even here:

```text
Human Direction
        ≠
Structural Truth
```

The Human may determine that Externalization should occur while the resulting asset may still contain unresolved structural problems.

Therefore authority over progression must not be confused with authority over truth.

---

# Emergence of the Authority Problem

The two-dimension candidate therefore exposes a third question:

```text
Reasoning-State Contribution
        ↓
What enters reasoning?


Reasoning-Process Influence
        ↓
What changes how reasoning proceeds?


???
        ↓
Who may determine or proceed
with what?
```

This missing dimension cannot be fully explained by Contribution or Influence.

---

# Initial Authority Candidate

A provisional third dimension is therefore:

> **Authority / Operational Latitude Boundary**

This is not yet a universal authority model.

It is a boundary mechanism.

Its purpose is to distinguish:

```text
Influence
```

from:

```text
Authority
```

and:

```text
Authority
```

from:

```text
Operational Latitude
```

---

# Authority

For the present operational analysis, Authority refers narrowly to:

> **the capacity, within the current reasoning arrangement, to make or establish a decision, constraint, transition, or permission that is treated as governing the relevant operation.**

This definition is intentionally narrow.

It does not attempt to define:

- legal authority
- moral authority
- social authority
- institutional authority
- epistemic supremacy

---

# Operational Latitude

Operational Latitude refers to:

> **the bounded range within which a participant may continue reasoning operations without requiring a new explicit decision at every micro-step.**

This distinction emerged strongly during Accelerated Mode.

# Why Operational Latitude Matters

During 03–06, the Human repeatedly entered a static or observational mode.

The AI then performed sequences such as:

```text
Candidate Formation
        ↓
Counter-test
        ↓
Compression
        ↓
Boundary Examination
        ↓
Restabilization Candidate
```

without the Human specifying every internal transition.

This would be difficult to represent if the only available categories were:

```text
Human controls
```

or:

```text
AI autonomous
```

A more accurate representation is:

```text
Bounded Operational Latitude
```

---

# Authority Is Not Micromanagement

Thus:

```text
Authority
        ≠
Micromanagement
```

A participant may retain authority while allowing another participant substantial Operational Latitude.

For example:

```text
Human retains relevant
decision capacity
        +
AI continues bounded
examination
```

can coexist.

---

# Latitude Is Not Authority Transfer

Likewise:

```text
Operational Latitude
        ≠
Authority Transfer
```

Allowing AI to perform several reasoning operations does not necessarily mean that the Human has transferred every relevant decision authority to AI.

---

# Latitude Is Not Unlimited Autonomy

Similarly:

```text
Operational Latitude
        ≠
Unlimited Autonomy
```

Latitude exists within:

- current objective
- current scope
- current constraints
- current Mission
- current stopping rules
- current responsibility boundaries

Therefore it is inherently bounded.

---

# Human Non-intervention Revisited

The Authority / Latitude distinction provides a stronger interpretation of explicit Human Non-intervention.

Instead of:

```text
Human silent
        ↓
AI has authority
```

the observed pattern can be represented as:

```text
Human does not intervene
        ↓
Existing Authority Boundary
remains unchanged
        +
Existing AI Operational Latitude
remains available
        ↓
Reasoning continues
```

This is a much narrower claim.

---

# Non-intervention as Boundary Maintenance

Thus explicit Non-intervention may be interpreted as:

> **maintenance of the current operational configuration**

rather than:

> **transfer of authority**

or:

> **unlimited delegation.**

This explains why the Human can remain in Observation Mode while still retaining the capacity to intervene.

---

# Initial Three-Dimension Candidate

The current Core Candidate therefore becomes:

```text
Human–AI Coupling
=
Reasoning-State Contribution
+
Reasoning-Process Influence
+
Authority / Operational Latitude Boundary
```

This is the first compact Operational Structure capable of explaining the observed Mission 002 interactions without preserving all initial interaction labels as separate mechanisms.

---

# Three Operational Questions

Each dimension answers a different question.

---

## Dimension 1

### Reasoning-State Contribution

> **What materially enters the current Reasoning State, and from whom?**

---

## Dimension 2

### Reasoning-Process Influence

> **What materially changes or maintains how the Reasoning Process proceeds?**

---

## Dimension 3

### Authority / Operational Latitude Boundary

> **Who may determine, constrain, permit, continue, or propose what within the current operational context?**

---

# Why the Three Dimensions Are Not Identical

The dimensions may overlap in a single interaction, but they do not collapse into one another.

A participant may:

```text
Contribute
without controlling
```

or:

```text
Influence
without possessing authority
```

or:

```text
Retain authority
without micromanaging
```

or:

```text
Exercise latitude
without receiving full authority
```

Therefore:

```text
Contribution
        ≠
Influence
        ≠
Authority
        ≠
Latitude
```

although Authority and Latitude are currently represented together as a boundary relation rather than two independent Core Mechanisms.

---

# Why Authority and Latitude Are Kept Together

It would be possible to create:

```text
Dimension 3 — Authority

Dimension 4 — Operational Latitude
```

However, the current evidence does not yet require that expansion.

The operationally important question is their relationship:

```text
What authority is retained?
        ↓
What latitude is permitted?
        ↓
Where is the boundary?
```

Therefore the more economical representation is:

> **Authority / Operational Latitude Boundary**

until evidence requires further decomposition.

---

# Anti-Proliferation Principle

This decision follows a recurring Mission 002 discipline:

> **Do not create an independent mechanism when a boundary relation sufficiently preserves the observed distinction.**

Thus:

```text
Authority
+
Latitude
```

are preserved as distinct concepts,

but:

```text
Authority Mechanism
+
Latitude Mechanism
```

are not separately created at this stage.

# Symmetry Question

The candidate also creates another important question.

Human and AI can both:

```text
Contribute to Reasoning State
```

and:

```text
Influence Reasoning Process
```

Does this mean Human and AI are operationally symmetric?

The immediate answer is:

> **Not necessarily.**

The shared dimensions do not imply identical authority, latitude, reliability, or responsibility.

---

# Coupling Does Not Imply Symmetry

Therefore:

```text
Coupling
        ≠
Symmetry
```

Human and AI may participate in the same Reasoning State while occupying different positions within the Authority / Latitude Boundary.

This distinction must survive the later examination.

---

# Shared Participation Does Not Imply Shared Responsibility

Similarly:

```text
Shared Participation
        ≠
Shared Responsibility
```

Both participants may contribute to a reasoning episode without possessing identical responsibility for:

- final decision
- validation
- execution
- stopping
- continuation
- Externalization
- Freeze
- downstream action

Thus participation and responsibility must not be collapsed.

---

# Shared Reasoning State Does Not Imply Shared Authority

Likewise:

```text
Shared Reasoning State
        ≠
Shared Authority
```

The current Reasoning State may contain Human and AI contributions simultaneously while authority remains asymmetric or context-dependent.

---

# Source-Neutrality and Source-Sensitivity

The emerging model should be:

> **source-neutral with respect to eligibility for reasoning contribution**

but not:

> **source-blind with respect to interpretation, reliability, authority, or provenance.**

Therefore:

```text
Human Input
AI Input
        ↓
Both may enter
Reasoning State
```

does not imply:

```text
Human Input
=
AI Input
```

in every evaluative dimension.

---

# Materiality Principle

The relevant question for Reasoning-State Contribution is:

> **Does this contribution materially affect the current reasoning problem?**

not merely:

> Who said it?

However, source may itself become materially relevant when evaluating:

- evidence quality
- reliability
- provenance
- authority
- validation requirement

Thus:

```text
Source-Neutral Eligibility
        ≠
Source-Blind Evaluation
```
