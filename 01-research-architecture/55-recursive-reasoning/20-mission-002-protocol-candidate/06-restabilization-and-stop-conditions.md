# Purpose

This document externalizes the Restabilization mechanism observed during Mission 002.

Its purpose is to specify what must occur after recursive examination and Structural Disposition have produced an updated reasoning structure before that reasoning state can be treated as sufficiently stable for subsequent operation.

The central operational question is:

> **After a Structurally Updated State has been produced, what conditions are sufficient to judge that the Reasoning State has Restabilized?**

This document does not attempt to define complete reasoning stability.

It does not require every unresolved question to disappear.

It does not define permanent stability.

It does not assume that Restabilization means returning to the reasoning state that existed before recursive re-entry.

Instead, it addresses a narrower operational problem:

> **How can materially relevant consequences of recursive change be sufficiently integrated so that no unresolved material instability prevents the reasoning process from entering a stable next operational state?**

# Operational Position

These are related but distinct operational responsibilities.

---

# Core Distinction

The foundational distinction of this document is:

> **Structurally Updated State ≠ Restabilized State**

A reasoning structure may have been correctly revised without the consequences of that Revision having been adequately integrated.

For example:

```text
Original Structure

A
↓
B
↓
C
```

Recursive examination produces:

```text
B → Revise
```

05 therefore produces:

```text
A
↓
B'
↓
C
```

This structure is updated.

However, several questions remain:

```text
B' → C ?
B' → Current Finding ?
B' → Current Objective ?
```

Until materially relevant consequences of B' have been sufficiently examined and integrated, the reasoning state cannot automatically be treated as Restabilized.

Therefore:

> **Structural Update is a prerequisite input to Restabilization, not evidence that Restabilization has already occurred.**

---

# Initial Restabilization Candidate

The initial candidate formed during bounded examination was:

```text
Structurally Updated State
        ↓
Consequences Integrated
        ↓
Dependencies Coherent
        ↓
No Material Unresolved Instability
        ↓
Reasoning Can Continue
        ↓
Restabilized State
```

This candidate provided the starting structure.

However, each element required counter-testing.

The resulting mechanism became substantially narrower than the initial formulation.

---

# Restabilization Is Not Complete Resolution

A strong initial interpretation would be:

> **Restabilization requires all unresolved questions to be resolved.**

This does not survive counter-testing.

Consider:

```text
Current Reasoning Structure

A ✓
↓
B' ✓
↓
C ✓

Peripheral Question P ?
Peripheral Question Q ?
```

P and Q may remain unresolved without materially affecting the current reasoning objective or the relevant dependency structure.

If their mere existence prevented Restabilization, then:

```text
Restabilization
        =
Complete Resolution
```

This would make the operational condition unnecessarily strong.

Reasoning frequently continues while non-material questions remain open.

Therefore:

> **No Unresolved Question**

is rejected as a Restabilization requirement.

The stronger surviving distinction is:

> **No Material Unresolved Instability**

---

# Materiality

The Restabilization mechanism therefore depends on material relevance.

Not every unresolved element prevents Restabilization.

The relevant question is:

> **Does the unresolved element materially affect the current reasoning structure, dependency relations, objective, or ability to enter a structurally valid next operational state?**

This maintains continuity with the Material Structural Consequence judgment formed in 05.

The relationship is:

```text
05
Material Structural Consequence
        ↓
Determines whether
Preservation / Revision
is required

06
Material Unresolved Instability
        ↓
Determines whether
Restabilization
has been achieved
```

The two judgments are related but serve different responsibilities.

---

# Restabilization Is Not Return to the Previous State

The term Restabilization may suggest:

```text
Stable State A
        ↓
Perturbation
        ↓
Recursive Examination
        ↓
Revision
        ↓
Return to Stable State A
```

This interpretation is rejected.

Meaningful recursive examination may alter the reasoning structure.

The resulting stable state may therefore differ materially from the state that existed before recursive re-entry.

A more accurate sequence is:

```text
Stable State A
        ↓
Recursive Perturbation
        ↓
Recursive Examination
        ↓
Preservation / Revision
        ↓
Updated State B
        ↓
Restabilization
        ↓
Stable State B
```

Therefore:

> **Restabilization ≠ Return to Previous State**

Instead:

> **Restabilization is the recovery of sufficient reasoning stability after recursive examination and Structural Disposition.**

---

# Restabilization Is Not Revision Recovery Only

A second possible interpretation is:

> Restabilization is required only when Revision occurred.

This also fails.

Consider:

```text
Earlier Finding
        ↓
Recursive Re-entry
        ↓
Recursive Examination
        ↓
Preserve
```

No Revision occurs.

However, recursive re-entry may have reopened:

- earlier assumptions
- dependency relations
- current findings
- current objectives
- structural relations between earlier and later reasoning

The Preserve judgment establishes that the examined structure remains adequate.

But that judgment must still be integrated into the current reasoning state.

Therefore Restabilization is not exclusively:

> **post-Revision recovery**

It is more generally:

> **post-recursive integration after Structural Disposition**

whether the disposition involved Preservation, Revision, or a component-sensitive combination of both.

---

# Restabilization as Operational Transition

The preceding counter-tests weaken the interpretation of Restabilization as a static classification.

The stronger interpretation is:

> **Restabilization is an operational transition from a recursively perturbed reasoning state to a sufficiently integrated reasoning state.**

The sequence is:

```text
Reasoning State
        ↓
Meaningful Recursive Trigger
        ↓
Recursive Re-entry
        ↓
Target / Depth Selection
        ↓
Recursive Examination
        ↓
Preservation / Revision
        ↓
Structurally Updated State
        ↓
Restabilization
        ↓
Restabilized State
```

Restabilization therefore operates after structural change or structural reaffirmation has occurred.

---

# Initial Three-Condition Candidate

Early examination produced three candidate conditions:

```text
A. Relevant Consequence Integration

B. Material Coherence

C. Continuation Readiness
```

The provisional formulation was:

```text
Relevant Consequences
Integrated
        +
Material Coherence
        +
Continuation Readiness
        ↓
Restabilization
```

These conditions were not accepted directly.

They were subjected to compression and counter-testing.

---

# Relevant Consequence Integration

The first condition concerns the effects of Structural Disposition.

Suppose:

```text
A
↓
B
↓
C
↓
D
```

and 05 produces:

```text
B → B'
```

The immediate updated structure is:

```text
A
↓
B'
↓
C
↓
D
```

The Revision may affect:

```text
B' → C
B' → D
B' → Current Finding
B' → Current Objective
```

Restabilization requires materially relevant consequences to be identified and sufficiently integrated.

This does not require examining every theoretically possible consequence.

It requires examination of consequences that are materially relevant to the current reasoning context.

---

# Sufficient Integration

The mechanism therefore uses:

> **Sufficient Integration**

rather than:

> complete integration of every possible consequence.

The relevant judgment is:

```text
Relevant Consequences
        ↓
Materially Relevant?
        │
        ├── No
        │     ↓
        │ Does Not Block
        │ Current Restabilization
        │
        └── Yes
              ↓
        Sufficiently Integrated
        for Current Context?
```

This is a context-sensitive judgment.

Mission 002 does not currently support a universal numerical threshold for sufficient integration.

The mechanism therefore specifies:

> where the judgment occurs

and:

> what distinction must be preserved

without claiming universal deterministic measurement.

---

# Integration ≠ Coherence

Relevant Consequence Integration and Material Coherence initially appeared potentially redundant.

They are not.

Consider:

```text
B → B'
        ↓
B' affects C
        ↓
Effect correctly identified
        ↓
B' conflicts with C
```

The relevant consequence has been identified and integrated into the reasoning process.

However:

> Material Coherence has not been restored.

Thus:

> **Relevant Consequence Integration ≠ Material Coherence**

Integration can expose incoherence.

It does not guarantee its absence.

---

# Coherence ≠ Sufficient Integration

The reverse also fails.

Suppose:

```text
B → B'

Observed:
B' ↔ C = coherent

Unexamined:
B' → D
```

No incoherence has yet been detected.

However, if D is materially relevant and has not been examined, the reasoning process cannot infer:

> Material Coherence established

merely from:

> no contradiction detected so far.

Therefore:

> **Absence of detected incoherence ≠ sufficient consequence integration**

Relevant Consequence Integration and Material Coherence remain operationally distinguishable.

---

# Relevant Structural Integration

The two conditions can be descriptively grouped under:

> **Relevant Structural Integration**

with:

```text
Relevant Structural Integration
        │
        ├── Relevant consequences
        │   sufficiently examined
        │
        └── Material dependencies
            sufficiently coherent
```

However, they should not be collapsed into one indistinguishable judgment.

The distinction between:

> **not yet examined**

and:

> **examined and found materially incoherent**

is operationally important.

Therefore the two dimensions remain separately visible inside the Restabilization mechanism.

---

# Material Coherence

Material Coherence does not require perfect global consistency across every possible reasoning element.

It requires:

> **the absence of unresolved incoherence that materially destabilizes the relevant current reasoning state.**

This can be expressed as:

```text
Relevant Dependencies
        ↓
Re-evaluated after
Structural Disposition
        ↓
Material Unresolved
Incoherence?
        │
        ├── Yes
        │     ↓
        │ Not Yet Restabilized
        │
        └── No
              ↓
        Restabilization
        remains possible
```

This is a sufficiency judgment rather than a completeness requirement.

---

# Local Validity ≠ Broader Coherence

A locally justified Revision may still produce broader instability.

For example:

```text
B → B'
```

may be locally correct.

But:

```text
B' → C
```

may no longer be coherent.

Therefore:

> **Local Structural Validity ≠ Broader Reasoning Coherence**

05 establishes the former through Structural Disposition.

06 examines the latter through consequence integration and Material Coherence.

---

# Forward Consequence Propagation

This produces a directional distinction.

Earlier recursive examination commonly moves backward:

```text
Current Problem
        ↓
Recursive Re-entry
        ↓
Earlier Target
        ↓
Depth
        ↓
Earlier Structure
```

After Structural Disposition, Restabilization may require forward examination:

```text
Revised / Preserved Structure
        ↓
Later Dependencies
        ↓
Current Finding
        ↓
Current Reasoning State
```

This is:

> **Consequential Forward Propagation**

It should not be confused with Recursive Depth.

---

# Backward Examination vs. Forward Integration

The two directions can be represented as:

```text
BACKWARD

Current Recursive Problem
        ↓
Target
        ↓
Depth
        ↓
Earlier Reasoning
        ↓
Examination


STRUCTURAL DISPOSITION

Earlier Reasoning
        ↓
Preserve / Revise


FORWARD

Updated Structure
        ↓
Relevant Consequences
        ↓
Affected Dependencies
        ↓
Current Reasoning State
        ↓
Restabilization
```

Thus:

> **Recursive Depth regulates backward examination.**

> **Restabilization evaluates materially relevant forward consequences of the resulting disposition.**

---

# Continuation Readiness Candidate

The initial third condition was:

> **Continuation Readiness**

meaning:

> Reasoning can continue after recursive examination.

This formulation was found to be too narrow.

A reasoning process may be correctly Restabilized even when it cannot continue along exactly the same path as before.

For example:

```text
Original

Assumption A
        ↓
Finding B
        ↓
Objective C
```

Recursive examination may produce:

```text
A → A'
```

and:

```text
A' no longer supports B
```

The reasoning structure may now be coherent.

But the original path toward C may no longer be valid.

This does not necessarily indicate instability.

It may indicate that the reasoning process should:

- redirect
- revise its objective
- select another valid path
- terminate the current path

Therefore:

> **Continuation Readiness ≠ ability to resume the previous reasoning path**

---

# Operational Readiness Candidate

Continuation Readiness was therefore refined into:

> **Operational Readiness**

meaning:

> **the updated reasoning state supports a structurally valid next reasoning operation.**

Potential next operations may include:

```text
Continue
Redirect
Revise Objective
Re-enter
Suspend
Terminate
```

This list is illustrative rather than a fixed action taxonomy.

The important distinction is:

> Restabilization does not require continuation in the same direction.

It requires that the reasoning state no longer be structurally blocked by unresolved material instability.

---

# Valid Termination

Termination provides an important counter-example.

Suppose recursive examination establishes:

> the current reasoning path is no longer valid.

The resulting sequence may be:

```text
Updated State
        ↓
Relevant Consequences Integrated
        ↓
Material Coherence Established
        ↓
Current Path Invalid
        ↓
Terminate Path
```

The reasoning process does not continue along the original path.

Yet the state may be fully Restabilized.

Therefore:

> **Valid Termination is compatible with Restabilization.**

This further weakens Continuation Readiness as an independent condition.

---

# Operational Readiness Counter-Test

The next question was:

> **Is Operational Readiness an independent Restabilization condition?**

To be independent, the following state should be stably possible:

```text
Relevant Consequences
Sufficiently Integrated = YES

Material Coherence = YES

Operational Readiness = NO
```

Counter-testing did not produce a stable independent case.

When Operational Readiness appeared absent, the reason could generally be decomposed into one of four conditions.

---

# Case 1 — Relevant Information Is Still Missing

If no valid next operation can be determined because materially relevant information or consequence analysis remains incomplete, then:

> Relevant Consequence Integration is not yet sufficient.

The failure returns to the first Core Condition.

---

# Case 2 — Material Incoherence Remains

If no valid next operation can be determined because dependencies remain structurally contradictory, then:

> Material Coherence has not been established.

The failure returns to the second Core Condition.

---

# Case 3 — Multiple Valid Actions Exist

If several next operations are available:

```text
Option A
Option B
Option C
```

the state is not necessarily unstable.

It may simply require:

> Action Selection

after Restabilization.

Therefore:

> **Multiple valid next actions ≠ failure of Restabilization**

---

# Case 4 — Current Path Should Terminate

If no continuation of the current path is justified, but termination itself is structurally justified, the state may still be Restabilized.

Therefore:

> **No continuation of current path ≠ Operationally unstable**

---

# Operational Readiness as Output Property

The independent-condition hypothesis therefore weakened.

The stronger model is:

```text
Relevant Consequences
Sufficiently Integrated
        +
No Material Unresolved
Incoherence
        ↓
Restabilized State
        ↓
Operational Readiness
```

Thus:

> **Operational Readiness is not retained as a third independent Restabilization condition.**

Instead:

> **Operational Readiness is treated as a property recovered by successful Restabilization.**

---

# Core Conditions after Compression

The Restabilization mechanism therefore compresses to two principal dimensions:

```text
A. Relevant Consequence Integration

B. Material Coherence
```

The relationship is sequential and mutually informative:

```text
Identify Relevant Consequences
        ↓
Sufficiently Integrate Them
        ↓
Re-evaluate Relevant Dependencies
        ↓
Material Unresolved Instability?
```

Operational Readiness follows when these conditions are sufficiently satisfied.

---

# Core Restabilization Statement

The current Core Candidate can be expressed as:

> **A Reasoning State is Restabilized when materially relevant consequences of recursive examination and Structural Disposition have been sufficiently integrated such that no unresolved material instability remains within the relevant current reasoning context.**

A more operational formulation is:

> **Integrate the materially relevant consequences of the updated reasoning structure, re-evaluate affected dependencies, and treat the state as Restabilized when no unresolved material instability remains that blocks structurally valid subsequent reasoning operation.**

---

# Completeness vs. Sufficiency

A major distinction formed during examination is:

```text
Completeness
        ≠
Sufficiency
```

Restabilization is not a Completeness Test.

It is a Sufficiency Test.

Not:

```text
Has everything been resolved?
        ↓
Yes / No
```

But:

```text
Have materially relevant
recursive consequences been
sufficiently integrated?
        ↓
Does material unresolved
instability remain?
        ↓
Yes / No
```

This prevents Restabilization from becoming an impossible requirement for total resolution.

---

# Restabilization Threshold

Reasoning stability may theoretically exist on a continuum.

For example:

```text
Highly Stable
Moderately Stable
Weakly Stable
Unstable
```

Mission 002 does not currently require a quantitative model of this continuum.

The operational problem is narrower:

> **Has the reasoning state crossed the threshold required to leave the current recursive instability and enter a valid next operational state?**

Therefore the current mechanism uses:

> **context-sensitive sufficiency judgment**

rather than:

> numerical stability measurement.

---

# No Universal Stability Threshold

The current evidence does not support a universal threshold such as:

```text
Restabilized if
Coherence Score > X
```

or:

```text
Restabilized if
Unresolved Dependencies < Y
```

Such thresholds would introduce unsupported precision.

Instead, Restabilization depends on:

- current reasoning objective
- materially relevant dependencies
- scope of recursive examination
- consequences of Structural Disposition
- unresolved material instability
- current operational context

Therefore:

> **Restabilization remains a context-sensitive structural judgment.**

---

# Not Yet Restabilized

The mechanism requires an explicit negative state:

> **Not Yet Restabilized**

This occurs when:

```text
Relevant consequences
not sufficiently integrated
```

or:

```text
Material unresolved instability
remains
```

The state does not imply failure of the entire recursive process.

It means:

> **the current recursive episode has not yet recovered sufficient stability.**

---

# Restabilization Failure ≠ Revision Operation

Suppose Restabilization examination discovers:

```text
B'
↓
C
```

is materially incoherent.

06 may conclude:

> **Not Yet Restabilized**

However, 06 does not automatically determine:

> how C should be revised.

That would reintroduce the Structural Disposition responsibility of 05.

Therefore:

```text
06
Material Incoherence Detected
        ↓
Not Yet Restabilized
```

does not equal:

```text
06
Determine Revision of C
```

This boundary is essential.

---

# Recursive Routing

A Not Yet Restabilized judgment may create the need for further recursive processing.

Conceptually:

```text
06
Not Yet Restabilized
        ↓
Further Recursive Processing Required
        ↓
Possible Re-examination /
Structural Disposition
```

The exact routing mechanism is not fully specified here.

It may involve responsibilities already represented in:

- 03 — Recursive Trigger and Re-entry
- 04 — Recursive Target and Depth
- 05 — Preservation and Revision

The present document does not create a new routing mechanism solely to resolve this question.

---

# Restabilization Is Episode-Local

Restabilization should not be interpreted as permanent global stability.

A useful descriptive formulation is:

> **Episode-local Restabilization**

meaning:

> the material instability associated with the current recursive episode has been sufficiently processed.

This does not imply:

- all future reasoning is stable
- no new evidence can appear
- no future Recursive Trigger can occur
- the reasoning structure is permanently frozen

Therefore:

```text
Restabilized
≠
Final

Restabilized
≠
Complete

Restabilized
≠
Immutable

Restabilized
≠
Never Re-enter
```

The term Episode-local is descriptive and does not currently require independent mechanism status.

---

# Future Recursive Trigger

A Restabilized State may later encounter new information.

The sequence may therefore be:

```text
Restabilized State
        ↓
Reasoning Continues
        ↓
New Observation
        ↓
Meaningful Recursive Trigger?
        │
        ├── No
        │     ↓
        │ Continue
        │
        └── Yes
              ↓
        Recursive Re-entry
```

This is fully compatible with 03.

Restabilization closes the current instability.

It does not eliminate future revisability.

The answer is:

> **No.**

> What should change?

> What follows from the change, and is the resulting state sufficiently coherent?

For example:

```text
05

B → B'
```

followed by:

```text
06
```

# Cause Does Not Determine Responsibility Placement

Revision may cause the need for Restabilization.

However:

> **the cause of an operation does not determine where the resulting responsibility belongs.**

For example:

```text
05
Revision
        ↓
causes
        ↓
06
Consequential Re-evaluation
```

This does not mean Consequential Re-evaluation should be absorbed into 05.

Likewise:

```text
03
Re-entry Required
        ↓
causes
        ↓
04
Target / Depth Selection
```

without making Target / Depth part of 03.

This maintains responsibility separation across Mission 002.

# Human–AI Boundary Problem

The downstream boundary is more delicate because:

> **07 — Human–AI Coupling**

follows Restabilization.

Mission 002 itself has been developed through Human–AI dialogue.

Human observations can materially alter recursive reasoning.

Therefore two problematic extremes must be avoided.

---

# Extreme A — AI-Internal Restabilization Only

One possible model is:

```text
AI Reasoning State
        ↓
Internal Coherence
        ↓
Restabilized
```

This is insufficient for the current operational context.

A Human observation may expose a materially relevant dependency that has not been integrated.

For example:

```text
AI
Restabilization Candidate
        ↓
Human Observation
        ↓
Previously unexamined
material dependency
        ↓
Restabilization Candidate
destabilized
```

Therefore:

> **AI-internal coherence alone cannot universally define coupled reasoning stability.**

---

# Extreme B — Explicit Human Approval Required

The opposite model is:

```text
AI Restabilization Candidate
        ↓
Human Review
        ↓
Explicit Human Approval
        ↓
Restabilized
```

This is also too strong.

Human participation may legitimately take the form of:

- observation
- silence
- selective intervention
- deferred review

Explicit Human confirmation is not required for every local structural judgment.

Furthermore:

> Human approval does not itself establish structural coherence.

Therefore:

> **Human Approval ≠ Restabilization**

---

# Human Agreement Does Not Define Structural Stability

A Human participant may disagree with a structurally coherent reasoning state.

A Human participant may also mistakenly accept a structurally incoherent state.

Therefore:

> **agreement and structural stability are different dimensions.**

Human input may affect the evidence available to Restabilization.

But Human agreement does not define Restabilization itself.

---

# Human Observation Can Affect Restabilization

Although Human approval is not required, Human observation may be materially relevant.

For example:

```text
Restabilization Candidate
        ↓
Human Observation:
"Dependency X has not been examined."
```

If X materially affects the current reasoning state:

```text
Relevant Consequences
not sufficiently integrated
        ↓
Not Yet Restabilized
```

Therefore Human input cannot simply be ignored.

The mechanism requires a way to accept materially relevant observations without absorbing the entire Human–AI Coupling responsibility.

---

# Source-Neutral Restabilization

The resulting solution is:

> **Restabilization should be source-neutral with respect to materially relevant observations.**

The relevant distinction is not:

```text
Human
vs.
AI
```

The relevant distinction is:

```text
Materially Relevant
vs.
Non-Material
```

Thus:

```text
AI Observation
        │
        ├── Materially Relevant
        │        ↓
        │     Integrate
        │
        └── Non-Material
                 ↓
            Does Not Block


Human Observation
        │
        ├── Materially Relevant
        │        ↓
        │     Integrate
        │
        └── Non-Material
                 ↓
            Does Not Block
```

This allows Human–AI input without turning 06 into a Coupling Architecture.

---

# Source-Neutral Does Not Mean Source-Blind

Source-neutrality does not mean that the origin of an observation can never matter.

The origin may affect:

- reliability
- interpretation
- responsibility
- interaction dynamics
- validation
- intervention behavior

However, those concerns do not define the Core Restabilization condition.

The Core question remains:

> **Does this information materially affect the current reasoning-state stability?**

Therefore:

> **source-neutral Restabilization does not imply source-blind reasoning.**

---

# Coupled Input ≠ Coupling Responsibility

This distinction can be compressed as:

> **06 may receive materially relevant input generated through Human or AI participation without becoming responsible for explaining the Human–AI coupling that generated that input.**

Therefore:

```text
Coupled Input
        ≠
Coupling Responsibility
```

# Consequence Identification

The first major operation in 06 is:

> **Identify Materially Relevant Consequences**

This does not mean enumerate every possible downstream implication.

The operational task is bounded.

A consequence becomes relevant when the Structural Disposition may materially affect:

- a dependency required by the current reasoning state
- a finding currently being relied upon
- an assumption supporting current reasoning
- a structural relationship
- the current objective
- the validity of a later conclusion
- a current operational decision

The exact set depends on context.

---

# Direct Consequences

The simplest case is a direct dependency.

```text
B → B'
        ↓
C depends directly on B
```

Then:

```text
B' → C
```

is an obvious consequence requiring evaluation.

This can be represented as:

```text
Changed Element
        ↓
Direct Dependent Element
        ↓
Re-evaluate
```

Direct consequences normally have high priority because their structural relationship is explicit.

---

# Indirect Consequences

Some consequences propagate through multiple dependencies.

For example:

```text
A
↓
B'
↓
C
↓
D
```

If B' changes the validity of C, D may also be affected.

Thus:

```text
B'
↓
C
↓
D
```

may require propagation beyond the direct dependent element.

However, this does not imply:

> propagate through the entire reasoning history.

The propagation remains bounded by material relevance.

---

# Consequence Propagation Boundary

The stopping question is:

> **Does further propagation remain materially relevant to the current reasoning state?**

Conceptually:

```text
Changed Element
        ↓
Dependent Element
        ↓
Materially Affected?
        │
        ├── No
        │     ↓
        │ Stop This Propagation Path
        │
        └── Yes
              ↓
        Integrate Consequence
              ↓
        Examine Next Relevant
        Dependency
```

This produces a bounded propagation process.

---

# Propagation Is Not Exhaustive Traversal

The mechanism does not require:

```text
Changed Element
        ↓
Every possible dependency
        ↓
Every downstream implication
        ↓
Entire reasoning history
```

Such exhaustive traversal would conflict with Recursive Economy.

Instead:

```text
Changed Element
        ↓
Materially Relevant
Dependency Paths
        ↓
Sufficient Integration
```

is preferred.

---

# Material Relevance as Propagation Control

Material relevance therefore serves two functions.

First:

> it determines whether an unresolved issue blocks Restabilization.

Second:

> it bounds consequence propagation.

Thus:

```text
Material Relevance
        ↓
Controls
        │
        ├── What must be examined
        │
        └── What may remain outside
            current Restabilization scope
```

This is important because without a propagation boundary, Restabilization could recreate unlimited recursive expansion.

---

# Consequence Integration

Identification alone is insufficient.

Suppose:

```text
B → B'
```

and the process identifies:

```text
B' affects C
```

but leaves the reasoning structure unchanged.

Then the consequence has been recognized but not integrated.

Integration requires the reasoning state to reflect the consequence appropriately.

Depending on the case, this may mean:

- reaffirming an existing dependency
- weakening a dependency
- recognizing that a dependency no longer holds
- marking an unresolved dependency
- propagating an updated finding
- recognizing that further recursive processing is required

Thus:

```text
Recognized
        ≠
Integrated
```

---

# Recognition vs. Integration

The distinction can be represented as:

```text
Observation:
"B' affects C"
        ↓
Recognition

        ↓

What does that effect
mean for current structure?
        ↓
Integration
```

Recognition is informational.

Integration is structural.

Restabilization requires the latter.

---

# Integration Does Not Always Mean Modification

Integration should not be confused with Revision.

A consequence may be integrated by confirming that no further structural modification is necessary.

For example:

```text
B → B'
        ↓
B' still supports C
        ↓
C remains valid
```

The consequence has been examined.

C may remain unchanged.

Therefore:

> **Integration ≠ Modification**

This prevents 06 from automatically generating unnecessary Revision.

---

# Integration Can Produce a New Instability

A consequence may also reveal:

```text
B'
↓
C
```

is no longer valid.

Then Integration may produce:

```text
Material Incoherence Detected
```

This does not mean the integration failed.

On the contrary:

> the integration successfully exposed a previously hidden structural problem.

Therefore:

```text
Successful Integration
        ↓
may produce
        ↓
Not Yet Restabilized
```

This is a valid outcome.

---

# Negative Findings Are Operationally Valuable

The Restabilization mechanism must therefore allow:

> **Not Yet Restabilized**

to be a successful judgment.

Otherwise the mechanism would be biased toward declaring stability.

For example:

```text
Relevant Consequences
Examined
        ↓
Material Conflict Found
        ↓
Not Yet Restabilized
```

is more structurally adequate than:

```text
Conflict Ignored
        ↓
Restabilized
```

Thus:

> **Restabilization examination succeeds when it correctly identifies the current stability state, not only when it produces a positive Restabilized judgment.**

---

# Material Coherence Evaluation

After materially relevant consequences have been sufficiently integrated, the process evaluates:

> **Does unresolved material instability remain?**

This is not identical to:

> Is every statement mutually consistent?

The scope is operational.

The relevant instability must materially affect the current reasoning state.

```text

```

# Material Incoherence vs. Uncertainty

Uncertainty is also not automatically instability.

A reasoning state may include:

```text

Moderate
```

while remaining structurally coherent.

Restabilization does not require maximal certainty.

The relevant question is:

> **Does the uncertainty materially prevent the current reasoning structure from supporting a valid next operation?**

If not, Restabilization may still be possible.

# Restabilization Under Uncertainty

```text
        ↓

        ↓

        ↓
```

# Operational Requirements Consolidation

The preceding examination supports a bounded set of Operational Requirements for Restabilization.

These requirements do not attempt to transform Restabilization into a universally deterministic algorithm.

They specify the minimum structural responsibilities that must be preserved if the mechanism is later operationalized as part of a Recursive Reasoning Protocol.

---

# Requirement 1 — Structural Disposition Must Precede Restabilization

Restabilization must not replace or prematurely overlap the Preservation / Revision responsibility.

The expected sequence is:

```text

        ↓
Structural Disposition
        ↓

        ↓

```

Therefore:

> **Restabilization must operate on the output of Structural Disposition rather than simultaneously deciding what should be preserved or revised.**

---

# Requirement 2 — Preservation Must Remain a Valid Entry State

Restabilization must not assume that Structural Disposition necessarily produced Revision.

The mechanism must accept:

```text
Preserve
```

as well as:

```text
Revise
```

and:

```text
Selective Preservation / Revision
```

as legitimate upstream outcomes.

Therefore:

> **Restabilization must operate after Structural Disposition generally, not only after Revision.**

---

# Requirement 3 — Relevant Consequences Must Be Identified

The mechanism must identify consequences of the Structural Disposition that are materially relevant to the current reasoning state.

It must not infer:

```text
Structural Update
        =
Consequences Already Integrated
```

Therefore:

> **A Structurally Updated State must be treated as an input requiring consequence examination rather than as automatic evidence of restored stability.**

---

# Requirement 4 — Consequence Propagation Must Be Bounded

The mechanism must not require exhaustive traversal of every theoretically possible downstream implication.

Instead:

```text

        ↓

        ↓

        │
        ├── Yes
        │     ↓
        │ Continue Integration
        │
        └── No
              ↓
        Stop This Path
```

Therefore:

> **Material relevance must bound consequence propagation.**

---

# Requirement 5 — Recognition Must Be Distinguished from Integration

The mechanism must distinguish:

```text
Consequence Recognized
```

from:

```text
Consequence Integrated
```

Recognizing that an updated structure affects another element is insufficient if the effect has not been incorporated into the current reasoning state.

Therefore:

> **Restabilization requires sufficient integration, not merely consequence detection.**

---

# Requirement 6 — Integration Must Not Imply Automatic Revision

A consequence may be integrated while the affected structure remains unchanged.

For example:

```text

        ↓

        ↓
C preserved
```

Therefore:

> **Consequence Integration must permit reaffirmation as well as further structural change.**

This protects the boundary between 05 and 06.

Therefore:

```text
Re-evaluated
        ↓

Instability?
```

must remain part of the Core Mechanism.

when materially relevant consequences remain unexamined.

Therefore:

> **Material Coherence must be assessed only after sufficient Relevant Consequence Integration.**

The relevant distinction is:


# Requirement 10 — Uncertainty Must Not Automatically Prevent Restabilization

A reasoning state may remain:

> **Restabilization must not be equated with certainty.**

The relevant criterion is material structural stability within the current context.
