# Scope

The Protocol Candidate concerns recursive reasoning in which an already-developed Reasoning State becomes materially questionable and some part of prior reasoning may require structured re-entry, re-examination, selective revision, and subsequent restabilization.

The protocol addresses five principal operational questions:

```text
WHEN?
        ↓
Should recursive reasoning re-enter?

WHAT?
        ↓
What should be revisited?

HOW FAR?
        ↓
How deeply should reasoning re-enter?

WHAT CHANGES?
        ↓
What should be preserved
and what should be revised?

WHEN STABLE AGAIN?
        ↓
When has the updated
Reasoning State restabilized?
```

Across these questions, the protocol additionally addresses:

```text
WHO CONTRIBUTES?

WHO INFLUENCES?

WITH WHAT
AUTHORITY /
OPERATIONAL LATITUDE?
```

through the Human–AI Coupling mechanism.

---

# Protocol Boundary

The protocol does not require every new observation to trigger recursive reasoning.

It does not require every contradiction to cause complete reconstruction.

It does not require every Human or AI judgment to become structurally authoritative.

It does not require every revision to propagate without boundary.

It does not require perfect certainty before continuation.

It does not define general Human–AI interaction outside the Recursive Reasoning context.

---

# Governing Principle

The protocol is governed by a central principle:

> **Recursive reasoning should re-enter prior reasoning only when materially warranted, revisit only what is structurally implicated, revise only what requires revision, preserve what remains valid, propagate relevant consequences, and stop when the updated Reasoning State has regained sufficient material coherence for continuation.**

Human and AI may participate throughout this process without collapsing contribution, influence, authority, responsibility, or structural validity into one another.

# Protocol Entry

The protocol begins from an existing Reasoning State.

```text
Existing Reasoning State
        ↓
New Observation /
Evidence /
Contradiction /
Dependency Change /
Structural Tension
```

The existence of new information alone is not sufficient to require recursive re-entry.

The protocol therefore begins with a Trigger examination.

---

# Stage 1 — Recursive Trigger and Re-entry

## Primary Question

> **Has something materially changed such that prior reasoning should be re-entered?**

The first stage distinguishes:

```text
New Information
```

from:

```text
Meaningful Recursive Trigger
```

Not every new observation is a Recursive Trigger.

---

# Meaningful Trigger

A Meaningful Trigger exists when the new condition materially challenges, changes, or reopens reasoning that remains relevant to the current structure.

The Trigger must have a material relationship to the existing Reasoning State.

Therefore:

```text
Novelty
        ≠
Meaningful Trigger
```

and:

```text
Difference
        ≠
Meaningful Trigger
```

---

# Re-entry Judgment

Once a Meaningful Trigger is recognized, the protocol asks:

> **Does the Trigger require recursive re-entry?**

Possible outcomes are:

```text
Trigger
        ↓
No Re-entry Required
```

or:

```text
Trigger
        ↓
Re-entry Required
```

---

# No Re-entry Required

A Meaningful observation does not necessarily require recursive re-entry.

Re-entry may be unnecessary when the observation:

- does not materially affect prior reasoning;
- is already represented by the current structure;
- does not alter relevant dependencies;
- does not undermine an operative assumption;
- does not change a structurally relevant judgment;
- can be incorporated without reopening prior reasoning.

Therefore:

```text
Meaningful Observation
        ≠
Automatic Re-entry
```

---

# Re-entry Required

Re-entry becomes operationally warranted when the Trigger materially implicates prior reasoning that must be reconsidered before reliable continuation.

The output of Stage 1 is therefore:

```text
Re-entry Required
```

or:

```text
Re-entry Not Required
```

---

# Stage 1 Responsibility Boundary

Stage 1 owns:

```text
Trigger Recognition

Re-entry Judgment
```

It does not own:

```text
Target Selection

Depth Selection

Preserve / Revise Judgment

Restabilization Judgment
```

# Stage 2 — Recursive Target and Depth

When Re-entry is required, the protocol must determine:

```text
WHAT?
```

and:

```text
HOW FAR?
```

---

# Recursive Target

The Recursive Target is the smallest structurally appropriate prior reasoning unit whose reconsideration is necessary to resolve the Trigger.

The target should be selected according to structural implication rather than convenience.

Therefore:

```text
Most Recent Element
        ≠
Automatic Target
```

and:

```text
Most Visible Element
        ≠
Automatic Target
```

---

# Target Selection Principle

The governing principle is:

> **Re-enter the smallest prior reasoning unit that is sufficient to address the material structural implication of the Trigger.**

This protects against both:

```text
Under-targeting
```

and:

```text
Over-targeting
```

---

# Under-targeting

Under-targeting occurs when the selected target is too narrow to resolve the material structural issue.

This may leave the underlying dependency failure intact.

---

# Over-targeting

Over-targeting occurs when reasoning reopens a larger structure than the Trigger materially requires.

This may cause:

- unnecessary reconstruction;
- loss of valid prior reasoning;
- uncontrolled branch creation;
- avoidable complexity;
- destabilization beyond the affected region.

---

# Recursive Depth

Target Selection identifies:

```text
Where to re-enter
```

Depth Selection identifies:

```text
How far the re-examination must propagate
```

These are related but distinct operational judgments.

---

# Depth Selection Principle

The governing principle is:

> **Recursive Depth should extend only as far as materially required to restore the structural dependencies implicated by the Trigger.**

Therefore:

```text
Re-entry
        ≠
Return to Origin
```

and:

```text
Revision Need
        ≠
Full-history Re-examination
```

---

# Target / Depth Relationship

The relationship is:

```text
Trigger
        ↓
Target
        ↓
Affected Dependencies
        ↓
Required Depth
```

Target identifies the point of re-entry.

Depth defines the necessary range of recursive examination.

---

# Stage 2 Output

Stage 2 produces:

```text
Recursive Target
        +
Recursive Depth
```

These define the bounded region of structural re-examination.

# Stage 3 — Preservation and Revision

Once the relevant reasoning region has been re-entered, the protocol asks:

> **What remains structurally valid, and what requires revision?**

The governing principle is:

```text
Re-examination
        ≠
Automatic Revision
```

---

# Preservation Principle

Prior reasoning should be preserved when it remains materially coherent with:

- the new evidence;
- the corrected dependency structure;
- the current objective;
- the relevant downstream reasoning;
- the revised structural context.

Therefore:

```text
Re-entered
        ≠
Invalidated
```

---

# Revision Principle

Revision is required when the re-examined structure no longer remains materially coherent with the relevant evidence or dependencies.

Therefore:

```text
Revision
```

is driven by structural necessity rather than by the mere existence of a Trigger.

---

# Preserve / Revise Judgment

The basic operational distinction is:

```text
Re-examined Element
        ↓
Still Materially Valid?
        │
        ├── YES
        │     ↓
        │   Preserve
        │
        └── NO
              ↓
            Revise
```

---

# Selective Revision

Revision should remain selective where possible.

The protocol therefore prefers:

```text
Preserve valid structure
        +
Revise implicated structure
```

over:

```text
Reconstruct everything
```

---

# Selective Revision Principle

> **The scope of revision should correspond to the scope of material structural invalidation.**

Therefore:

```text
Local Failure
        ≠
Global Reconstruction
```

unless the dependency structure demonstrates that the failure is genuinely global.

---

# Preservation Is Active

Preservation is not equivalent to doing nothing.

Preservation is an operational judgment that the examined structure remains sufficiently valid.

Thus:

```text
Preserve
        ≠
Not Examined
```

---

# Revision Is Bounded

Revision does not automatically authorize unrestricted restructuring.

It remains bounded by:

```text
Recursive Target

Recursive Depth

Affected Dependencies

Current Objective

Current Responsibility
```

---

# Consequence Propagation

When revision occurs, the protocol must consider materially relevant consequences.

A revised element may affect:

```text
Dependencies

Assumptions

Intermediate Judgments

Downstream Structures

Operational Decisions
```

The protocol therefore requires relevant consequence propagation.

---

# Propagation Boundary

Not every possible consequence must be examined.

The governing principle is:

> **Propagate revision through materially relevant dependencies until the affected reasoning structure is sufficiently integrated for coherence examination.**

Therefore:

```text
Consequence Propagation
        ≠
Unlimited Re-examination
```

# Stage 4 — Restabilization

After revision and consequence integration, the protocol asks:

> **Has the updated Reasoning State become sufficiently stable for reliable continuation?**

Restabilization does not mean:

```text
Perfect Certainty
```

or:

```text
No Remaining Unknowns
```

or:

```text
Permanent Finality
```

---

# Restabilization Core

The current Restabilization mechanism is:

```text
Relevant Consequence Integration
        +
Material Coherence
        ↓
Restabilized Reasoning State
```

---

# Relevant Consequence Integration

The updated Reasoning State must incorporate the materially relevant consequences of the revision.

A state cannot be considered Restabilized merely because the initially revised element appears locally coherent.

Relevant downstream implications must also be sufficiently integrated.

---

# Material Coherence

After relevant consequences have been integrated, the resulting Reasoning State must no longer contain a known material contradiction that prevents reliable continuation.

Therefore:

```text
Material Coherence
        ≠
Perfect Completeness
```

and:

```text
Material Coherence
        ≠
Absence of Uncertainty
```

---

# Operational Readiness

Operational Readiness is treated as an output of sufficient:

```text
Relevant Consequence Integration
        +
Material Coherence
```

rather than as an independent third Restabilization condition.

Thus:

```text
Relevant Integration
        +
Material Coherence
        ↓
Operational Readiness
```

---

# Restabilization Judgment

The protocol may treat the Reasoning State as Restabilized when:

```text
Relevant consequences
have been sufficiently integrated
        +
No known material incoherence
blocks continuation
```

---

# Restabilization Is Provisional

A Restabilized State remains open to future Recursive Triggers.

Therefore:

```text
Restabilized
        ≠
Permanently Closed
```

A future material observation may reopen the cycle.

---

# Stage 4 Output

```text

```

```text
Continuation
```

until another Meaningful Trigger arises.
