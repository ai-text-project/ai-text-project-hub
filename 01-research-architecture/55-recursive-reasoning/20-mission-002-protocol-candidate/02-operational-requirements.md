# Purpose

This document identifies the operational requirements that emerged while attempting to transform the Recursive Reasoning Pattern v0.2 into a usable operational process.

The objective is not to define the final Recursive Reasoning Protocol.

The objective is not to prescribe a fixed sequence of actions.

Instead, this document records the capabilities and judgment functions that appear necessary if recursive reasoning is to be operationalized without reducing the observed pattern to a rigid procedural sequence.

The requirements documented here emerged through operationalization analysis, recursive dialogue, counter-testing, restabilization, placement analysis, and post-interval re-observation.

They therefore represent evidence-supported operational requirements within the current reasoning trajectory.

---

# Research Context

Mission 001 identified and progressively validated a recurring Recursive Reasoning Pattern.

The evidence-supported pattern included recurring operations such as:

- recursive trigger recognition
- re-entry
- recursive target selection
- selective recursive depth
- preservation and revision
- restabilization
- stop judgment

Mission 002 began with a different question.

The question was no longer:

> What pattern can be observed?

The operational question became:

> What must a reasoning process be capable of doing in order to enact this pattern reliably?

Initial analysis showed that the pattern itself does not fully specify the judgments required for operational execution.

For example:

- What counts as a meaningful recursive trigger?
- When does a trigger justify re-entry?
- Which prior reasoning state should become the recursive target?
- How deep should recursive examination proceed?
- What should be preserved?
- What should be revised?
- When has reasoning become sufficiently stable?
- When should additional reasoning stop?
- When is a reasoning state ready for externalization?

These questions exposed an operational gap between the observed pattern and a repeatable protocol.

---

# Core Operational Finding

The central finding of the operationalization analysis is:

> Recursive reasoning cannot be operationalized adequately as a fixed sequence of procedural steps alone.

The observed reasoning process repeatedly requires:

> **Context-sensitive judgment over the current reasoning state.**

The same apparent event may require different responses depending on:

- current reasoning phase
- current objective
- structural stability
- unresolved dependencies
- relevance of new evidence
- expected effect of further reasoning
- interaction between Human and AI reasoning states

Operationalization therefore requires both procedural structure and context-sensitive judgment.

---

# Operational Requirement 1

## Context-Sensitive Judgment

Recursive reasoning must be capable of evaluating events relative to the current reasoning state rather than treating them as context-independent signals.

A new observation does not automatically require recursive re-entry.

A contradiction does not automatically require revision.

Agreement does not automatically imply convergence.

Additional reasoning does not automatically improve the result.

The operational process must therefore evaluate:

> What does this event mean relative to the current reasoning state?

This requirement appears to operate across the entire recursive reasoning process.

It is not limited to a single stage.

---

# Operational Requirement 2

## Recursive Trigger Discrimination

The process must distinguish between:

- information that merely appears during reasoning
- information that materially affects the current reasoning state

A potential trigger should be evaluated according to whether it may:

- alter an existing finding
- expose an unresolved dependency
- reveal a contradiction
- destabilize an apparently stable structure
- require reconsideration of an earlier judgment

Therefore:

> Observation does not automatically imply recursive trigger.

A recursive trigger is operationally meaningful when the new information has sufficient structural relevance to justify reconsideration.

---

# Operational Requirement 3

## Re-entry Judgment

Even when a meaningful trigger is detected, recursive re-entry should not occur automatically.

The process must determine whether returning to an earlier reasoning state is necessary.

Possible outcomes include:

- absorb the new information without re-entry
- preserve the existing structure
- locally revise the current finding
- selectively re-enter an earlier reasoning state
- reopen a larger portion of the reasoning trajectory

Therefore:

> Trigger recognition and re-entry judgment are distinct operational functions.

---

# Operational Requirement 4

## Selective Recursive Target

When re-entry is required, the process must identify the appropriate recursive target.

Possible targets may include:

- a previous assumption
- an earlier finding
- a dependency
- a classification decision
- a structural interpretation
- a placement judgment
- an earlier reasoning phase

The process should avoid reopening the entire reasoning history when only a limited region requires reconsideration.

Therefore:

> Recursive reasoning requires selective target identification.

---

# Operational Requirement 5

## Selective Recursive Depth

Selecting the recursive target does not determine how deeply reasoning should proceed.

The process must separately determine recursive depth.

Recursive depth may remain local when:

- the issue is isolated
- surrounding findings remain stable
- no additional dependencies are affected

Greater depth may be required when:

- foundational assumptions are affected
- multiple findings depend on the target
- structural consistency is threatened
- earlier reasoning states must be reconstructed

Therefore:

> Recursive target and recursive depth are separate operational judgments.

Recursive reasoning should be selectively deep rather than uniformly deep.

---

# Operational Requirement 6

## Preservation and Revision Judgment

Recursive reasoning must distinguish between what should be revised and what should remain stable.

New evidence should not automatically reopen previously stabilized findings.

The operational process must evaluate whether new information:

- contradicts an existing finding
- refines an existing finding
- extends an existing finding
- creates a new branch
- can be absorbed without structural change
- requires revision

This produces a central operational distinction:

> **Re-entry does not imply revision.**

Recursive reasoning therefore requires an explicit preservation function in addition to a revision function.

---

# Operational Requirement 7

## Restabilization

After recursive reconsideration, the process must determine whether the reasoning structure has returned to a sufficiently stable state.

Restabilization does not necessarily mean returning to the previous structure.

A reasoning state may restabilize through:

- preservation
- refinement
- revision
- structural reorganization
- separation of previously mixed findings

The relevant question is:

> Has the reasoning state become structurally coherent enough to proceed?

Restabilization is therefore a distinct operational function following recursive examination.
