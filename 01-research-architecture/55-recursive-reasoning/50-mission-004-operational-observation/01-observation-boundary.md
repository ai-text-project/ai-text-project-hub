# Purpose

This document defines the evidence boundary governing Mission 004 — Operational Observation.

Mission 004 examines how the frozen Recursive Reasoning Protocol behaves when applied to a naturally occurring operational reasoning situation.

Operational use alone, however, does not produce interpretable evidence.

Without an explicit observation boundary, operational events may become mixed with:

- retrospective interpretation;
- prior expectations;
- known findings from Mission 003;
- protocol assessment;
- revision judgment;
- validation claims;
- or success-oriented case construction.

The purpose of this document is therefore to preserve the distinction between:

```text
Operational Event
        ↓
Observation
        ↓
Assessment
        ↓
Downstream Implication
```

These stages may inform one another sequentially.

They should not be treated as equivalent.

---

# Core Boundary

Mission 004 follows the governing distinction:

> **Observation ≠ Interpretation ≠ Assessment ≠ Revision**

More explicitly:

```text
What occurred
        ≠
What the occurrence may mean

What was observed
        ≠
How Protocol behavior is assessed

Protocol behavior assessment
        ≠
Protocol revision requirement

Operational evidence
        ≠
Protocol validation
```

Mission 004 should preserve these distinctions throughout operational observation.

---

# Layer A — Operational Event

An Operational Event is something that occurs during actual use of the frozen Protocol.

Examples may include:

- a possible recursive trigger appearing;
- difficulty determining whether re-entry is required;
- selection of a recursive target;
- expansion or restriction of examination depth;
- a Preserve / Revise judgment;
- a recursive return;
- difficulty determining a return route;
- attempted restabilization;
- inability to continue;
- use of reasoning not explicitly represented by the Protocol;
- or unexpected operational behavior.

At this layer, the responsibility is primarily descriptive.

The question is:

> **What happened during Protocol use?**

No Protocol-level conclusion is required at this stage.

---

# Layer B — Operational Observation

Operational Observation organizes the observed event sufficiently for later examination.

The question becomes:

> **What behavior of the Protocol was observable in relation to the event?**

Observation may identify:

- exercised mechanisms;
- non-exercised mechanisms;
- decision points;
- ambiguity;
- friction;
- recursive movement;
- stabilization behavior;
- termination;
- and unexpected behavior.

Observation should remain as close as reasonably possible to the operational event.

It should not silently convert an event into a causal explanation.

Therefore:

```text
Observed Friction
        ≠
Known Cause

Observed Ambiguity
        ≠
Protocol Defect

Observed Success
        ≠
Protocol Validity
```

---

# Layer C — Protocol Behavior Assessment

Protocol Behavior Assessment occurs after observation has been preserved.

Its responsibility is to evaluate what the observed behavior indicates about operational use of the Protocol.

Possible assessment categories may include:

```text
SUPPORTED BEHAVIOR

QUALIFIED BEHAVIOR

FRICTION

FAILURE

NOT EXERCISED

UNEXPECTED OBSERVATION
```

These categories describe the observed operational relation.

They do not by themselves authorize Protocol revision or validation claims.

Detailed assessment criteria will be defined later in Mission 004.

---

# Layer D — Downstream Implication

Only after observation and assessment may Mission 004 consider what the evidence implies for future research responsibility.

Possible downstream implications may include:

- Preserve;
- Qualify;
- Revision Candidate;
- Additional Operational Observation;
- Additional Independent Examination;
- Insufficient Information;
- or another research responsibility.

A downstream implication is not predetermined by the operational event.

Accordingly:

```text
Friction
   ↓
Observation
   ↓
Assessment
   ↓
Possible Revision Candidate
```

is valid.

The following shortcut is not:

```text
Friction
   ↓
Immediate Protocol Revision
```

---

# Frozen Protocol Boundary

Mission 004 observes the frozen Canonical Protocol Candidate produced during Mission 002.

The Protocol Object should not be silently modified during operational observation.

If an operational problem appears:

```text
Operational Problem
        ↓
RECORD
        ↓
Continue under Frozen Protocol
if operationally possible
        ↓
Assessment
```

If continued application becomes impossible or would materially distort the observation, non-completion may be preserved as an operational result.

The Protocol should not be repaired merely to allow the observation to reach a preferred conclusion.

Therefore:

> **Mid-observation repair is not part of Operational Observation.**

Any later revision requires a separate downstream judgment.

---

# Known-Finding Boundary

Mission 003 identified three principal downstream Revision Candidates:

```text
1. Materiality

2. Preserve → Restabilization explicitness

3. Recursive-return routing responsibility
```

These findings are already known before Mission 004 begins.

Mission 004 therefore cannot claim complete blindness to prior findings.

The appropriate protection is not artificial ignorance.

The appropriate protection is to prevent known findings from becoming forced observation targets.

Accordingly:

```text
Known Finding
        ↓
Do not construct the case
to reproduce it
        ↓
Does it naturally become
operationally relevant?
       / \
     YES  NO
      │    │
      ▼    ▼
   Record  No positive
           or negative
           inference
```

If a known finding naturally appears during operational use, it may be recorded.

If it does not appear, Mission 004 should not infer that the underlying issue has been resolved or disproven.

---

# Case Selection Boundary

Mission 004 requires an operational case in which recursive reconsideration is genuinely relevant.

The case should not be selected primarily because:

- the Protocol is expected to perform well;
- a preferred outcome is likely;
- a Mission 003 finding can be reproduced;
- the case conveniently exercises every Protocol mechanism;
- or the result is already known.

The governing distinction is:

```text
A reasoning situation naturally creates
a reason for recursive reconsideration
        ✓

A reasoning situation is created or selected
primarily to demonstrate the Protocol
        ✗
```

Detailed Operational Entry Conditions are defined separately in:

`02-operational-entry-conditions.md`

---

# Retrospective Reconstruction Boundary

Operational evidence should not be reconstructed solely after the outcome is known when contemporaneous preservation is reasonably possible.

Retrospective explanation can introduce:

- outcome bias;
- narrative smoothing;
- omitted uncertainty;
- artificial causal coherence;
- and selective memory.

Mission 004 should therefore preserve operational events and decisions as close as reasonably possible to the time they occur.

Later interpretation may be added.

It should remain distinguishable from the original observation record.

Thus:

```text
Contemporaneous Observation
        +
Later Interpretation
        ✓

Later Interpretation
presented as if it were
Contemporaneous Observation
        ✗
```

---

# Evidence–Interpretation Boundary

Mission 004 distinguishes evidence preservation from explanatory interpretation.

For example:

```text
Evidence:
"The Protocol did not specify an obvious return route
at this decision point."

Interpretation:
"This may correspond to the Recursive-return Routing
qualification identified during Mission 003."
```

Both statements may eventually be relevant.

They have different evidence responsibilities.

The first records operational behavior.

The second establishes a comparative interpretation.

They should not be silently merged.

---

# Non-Exercise Boundary

A Protocol mechanism may not be exercised during a particular operational case.

Non-exercise is not equivalent to successful operation.

Therefore:

```text
Mechanism not exercised
        ≠
Mechanism successfully supported

Mechanism not exercised
        ≠
Mechanism operationally refuted
```

The appropriate status is:

> **NOT EXERCISED**

This protects Mission 004 from making judgments about Protocol components that the case did not actually test through use.

---

# Success and Failure Boundary

Mission 004 does not equate smooth operational behavior with validation.

Likewise, operational difficulty does not automatically constitute Protocol rejection.

```text
Smooth Operational Behavior
        ≠
General Validity

Operational Friction
        ≠
Protocol Rejection

Operational Failure
        ≠
Research Failure
```

Operational failure may be highly informative evidence.

Mission success depends on valid observation and evidence preservation rather than preferred Protocol performance.

---

# Independent Evidence Boundary

Operational evidence and independent evidence are different evidence dimensions.

Mission 004 may produce operational evidence without producing independent evidence.

This distinction is especially important when the operational situation occurs within a Human–AI research relationship historically connected to the formation of the Protocol.

Therefore:

```text
Operational Evidence
        ≠
Independent Evidence

Operational Use
        ≠
Independent Examination
```

Mission 004 should describe the actual evidence relation rather than automatically assigning independence to operational observation.

---

# Human–AI Coupling Boundary

The Recursive Reasoning Protocol contains a Human–AI Coupling mechanism.

Operational use may therefore produce observations concerning Human and AI responsibilities.

However:

```text
Human–AI Interaction Observed
        ≠
Human–AI Coupling Validated

Responsibility Distribution Observed
        ≠
General Human–AI Architecture Established
```

Mission 004 may record operational responsibility behavior.

General claims about Human–AI Coupling require evidence beyond a single operational observation.

---

# Validation Boundary

Mission 004 does not begin with a validation claim.

The following claims are not established merely by operational observation:

- Protocol Validated;
- Independently Validated;
- Empirically Validated;
- Generally Valid;
- Human–AI Coupling Validated;
- Failure Protections Demonstrated Generally;
- or Universal Operational Applicability.

Any stronger evidence status must be separately justified by the evidence actually produced.

---

# Principal Observation Risks

Mission 004 should protect against at least the following risks.

## 1. Confirmation Bias

Interpreting operational behavior primarily as confirmation of the existing Protocol.

## 2. Success-oriented Case Selection

Selecting a case because successful Protocol performance is expected.

## 3. Known-Finding Search Bias

Actively searching for Mission 003 findings rather than observing whether they naturally become relevant.

## 4. Retrospective Reconstruction

Reconstructing operational events after the result is known and presenting the reconstruction as contemporaneous observation.

## 5. Mid-observation Protocol Repair

Changing the Protocol during observation to eliminate emerging friction.

## 6. Evidence–Interpretation Mixing

Presenting explanatory interpretation as if it were directly observed evidence.

## 7. Non-exercise Inflation

Treating mechanisms not exercised by the case as operationally supported.

## 8. Validation Escalation

Moving from one operational observation to stronger validation or general-validity claims without sufficient evidence.

## 9. Failure Escalation

Treating a local operational difficulty as immediate evidence that the entire Protocol should be rejected.

## 10. Independence Inflation

Treating operational evidence as independent merely because it occurs after Mission 003.

# Boundary Summary

Mission 004 preserves the following responsibility separation:

```text
OPERATIONAL EVENT
What happened?
        ↓
OBSERVATION
What Protocol behavior was observable?
        ↓
ASSESSMENT
What does that behavior indicate
within this operational case?
        ↓
DOWNSTREAM IMPLICATION
What future research responsibility,
if any, is justified?
```

No layer should silently substitute for another.

---

# Governing Boundary Principles

Mission 004 therefore operates under the following principles:

> **Observe before assessing.**

> **Assess before revising.**

> **Record known findings if they naturally appear; do not force them to appear.**

> **Do not repair the frozen Protocol during observation merely to preserve successful operation.**

> **Treat non-exercise as non-exercise.**

> **Treat operational evidence and independent evidence as distinct dimensions.**

> **Treat a single operational case as bounded evidence.**
