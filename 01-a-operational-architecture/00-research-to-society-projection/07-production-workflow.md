# Production Workflow

**Workflow Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the operational workflow through which Projection Designs are transferred into communication production.

The objective is not to prescribe how communication products are created.

Instead, the workflow establishes a consistent handover process from Projection Design to downstream production roles.

This separation enables communication production without altering the underlying Research Package or Projection Strategy.

---

# Position within the Projection Architecture

Production begins only after the Projection Layer has completed its strategic responsibilities.

The overall workflow is:

Research Space

↓

Navigation Layer

↓

Projection Layer

↓

Phase Transformation

↓

Media Assignment

↓

Production Workflow

↓

Communication Product

---

# Role of Production

Production is responsible for creating communication artifacts based on completed projection specifications.

Production does not redefine:

- research
- audience
- projection strategy
- transformation strategy
- media assignment

These decisions have already been completed upstream.

Production therefore focuses on execution rather than strategic design.

---

# Production Inputs

Production receives a completed projection specification.

Typical inputs include:

- Research Package
- audience definition
- projection objectives
- phase transformation strategy
- media assignment
- production requirements

These inputs establish a stable foundation for communication production.

---

# Production Activities

Typical production activities include:

- article creation
- presentation development
- visual production
- dialogue script generation
- educational material creation
- communication formatting

Production activities remain implementation-oriented.

---

# Production Outputs

Production generates communication products such as:

- Medium articles
- presentation slides
- dialogue demonstrations
- visual assets
- educational resources
- communication packages

Each product represents one implementation of the Projection Strategy.

---

# Relationship to Production Roles

Production may involve multiple specialized roles.

Examples include:

- Bridge Content Producer
- Visual Designer
- Presentation Designer
- Dialogue Designer
- Educational Content Designer

Each role contributes to implementation while preserving the upstream projection strategy.

---

# Workflow Independence

Production workflows may differ depending on communication objectives.

For example:

Research Package

↓

Projection Specification

↓

Presentation Development

or

Research Package

↓

Projection Specification

↓

Article Development

or

Research Package

↓

Projection Specification

↓

Dialogue Development

Although workflows differ, all originate from the same projection architecture.

---

# Quality Preservation

Throughout production, the following elements should remain consistent:

- research identity
- conceptual integrity
- methodological consistency
- projection objectives
- audience alignment

Production may refine expression but should not redefine the communication strategy.

---

# Future Expansion

The Production Workflow is intentionally modular.

New production roles and communication formats may be added without modifying the Navigation Layer, Projection Layer, or Research Packages.

This architecture supports continuous operational growth while maintaining structural consistency.

---

# Summary

The Production Workflow provides the operational bridge between Projection Design and communication implementation.

By separating strategic design from production execution, the architecture enables scalable communication development while preserving the integrity of the Research Program.
