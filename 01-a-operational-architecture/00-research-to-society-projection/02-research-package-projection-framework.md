# Research Package Projection Framework

**Framework Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the Research Package as the fundamental projection unit within the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

Rather than projecting individual papers or isolated research outputs, the Bridge Layer projects complete Research Packages that preserve the structural relationships among research assets.

The objective is to maintain methodological integrity while enabling multiple forms of social communication.

---

# Why Research Packages

Traditional science communication often treats individual papers as independent communication units.

The Dialogue-Phase Reasoning Research Program adopts a different approach.

Each completed research topic consists of multiple interconnected assets.

These assets collectively form a Research Package.

The Bridge Layer therefore projects the complete package rather than isolated documents.

---

# Definition of a Research Package

A Research Package is a structured collection of research assets representing one coherent research theme.

Typical components include:

- README
- Abstract
- Blueprint
- Figures
- Research Assets
- Dialogue Assets
- Presentation Assets
- Manuscript

Additional supporting materials may also be included depending on the research topic.

The exact composition of each package may evolve while preserving its structural coherence.

# Research Package as a Projection Unit

The Bridge Layer does not project individual files.

Instead, each Research Package becomes the smallest unit of projection.

A single Research Package may generate multiple communication pathways depending on audience and communication objectives.

This approach preserves structural consistency across different forms of communication.

---

# Internal Structural Relationships

A Research Package is composed of interconnected assets rather than isolated documents.

Examples include:

- conceptual overview
- methodological explanation
- research evidence
- visual representation
- implementation guidance

Each component supports the others.

Projection therefore preserves relationships among assets rather than selecting isolated documents.

---

# Projection Readiness

A completed Research Package serves as the canonical source for future communication.

Before projection begins, the package should satisfy basic structural completeness.

Typical indicators include:

- research documentation
- structural overview
- visual assets
- methodological description
- primary manuscript
- navigation support

The Bridge Layer assumes that completed packages are stable enough for projection.

---

# Projection Independence

Projection does not modify the Research Package.

The package remains the canonical research source.

Different communication products are generated from the package while leaving the original research unchanged.

This separation preserves research integrity and enables future projections to remain consistent.

---

# Relationship to Audience Architecture

The Research Package itself is audience-independent.

Audience adaptation begins only after the package enters the Projection Layer.

Different audiences therefore receive different projections of the same package.

The research itself remains identical.

---

# Relationship to Phase Transformation

The Research Package defines what is projected.

The Phase Transformation Strategy defines how it is projected.

These responsibilities remain intentionally separated.

---

# Relationship to Production

The Research Package is not a communication product.

It becomes the input for downstream production roles.

Examples include:

- Medium articles
- Enterprise presentations
- Dialogue demonstrations
- Educational materials
- Public research guides

Each production activity originates from the same Research Package.

---

# Summary

The Research Package Projection Framework establishes the Research Package as the canonical projection unit within the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

By projecting complete packages rather than isolated documents, the Bridge Layer preserves structural continuity, methodological integrity, and long-term consistency across multiple forms of communication.
