# Governance

**Governance Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the governance principles that preserve the structural integrity of the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

The objective is not to regulate individual communication activities.

Instead, the governance framework protects the long-term consistency of the operational architecture while allowing continuous expansion of communication methods, audiences, and production environments.

---

# Governance Philosophy

The Research-to-Society Projection Architecture is designed to evolve.

Governance therefore preserves architectural consistency rather than restricting operational flexibility.

Stable architectural principles enable adaptive communication.

---

# Core Governance Principles

The following principles should remain stable throughout future development.

- Research remains the canonical source.
- Projection never modifies research.
- Communication follows Projection Strategy.
- Production follows completed specifications.
- Roles preserve their defined responsibilities.
- Research Packages remain reusable across multiple communication environments.

These principles establish the foundation of the architecture.

---

# Preservation of the Research Space

The Research Space serves as the canonical repository of completed research.

Governance ensures that:

- research assets remain independent from communication products
- Research Packages preserve methodological integrity
- projection activities do not alter original research

The Research Space therefore remains the permanent reference point for all downstream activities.

---

# Preservation of Projection Architecture

The Projection Layer is responsible for communication design.

Governance ensures that:

- Projection remains independent from Production
- audience analysis precedes media selection
- phase transformation follows projection strategy
- communication objectives remain explicit

These principles preserve strategic consistency.

---

# Preservation of Role Boundaries

Operational responsibilities remain intentionally separated.

Governance protects the boundaries between:

- Research Space
- Bridge-Layer Strategist
- Phase Transformation Designer
- Bridge Content Producer

Role expansion is encouraged.

Role duplication is discouraged.

---

# Controlled Evolution

The architecture is expected to expand over time.

Examples include:

- new audiences
- new communication environments
- new production methods
- additional operational roles

Expansion should extend the architecture rather than redefine its foundations.

---

# Architectural Consistency

Future revisions should preserve the following characteristics:

- one canonical Research Space
- one Projection Architecture
- reusable Research Packages
- modular operational roles
- independent communication products

These characteristics maintain continuity across future development.

---

# Governance of Communication Products

Communication products are considered projections of completed research.

They should therefore:

- preserve conceptual integrity
- preserve methodological consistency
- preserve research identity

Communication products may differ in form without changing the underlying research.

---

# Long-term Sustainability

The governance framework supports continuous development while minimizing structural disruption.

New operational components should integrate with existing architecture through clearly defined interfaces rather than replacing established responsibilities.

This approach enables sustainable long-term evolution of the Research Program.

---

# Summary

Governance preserves the structural identity of the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

Rather than controlling communication itself, governance protects the relationships between Research Space, Projection, Transformation, and Production.

By maintaining these architectural principles, the Research Program can evolve continuously while preserving methodological integrity and operational consistency.
