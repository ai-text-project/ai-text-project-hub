# Research-to-Society Projection

**Architecture Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Overview

This directory contains the operational architecture that defines how completed Research Packages are projected from the Research Space into society.

Unlike the Research Space, which preserves research assets, the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture focuses on designing communication pathways that connect research with diverse audiences while preserving methodological integrity.

The architecture separates strategic planning, communication transformation, and production into independent operational layers, enabling sustainable long-term development.

---

# Purpose

The objective of this architecture is to establish a systematic framework for projecting completed research into multiple communication environments.

Rather than creating individual communication products, the architecture defines:

- how Research Packages are selected
- how audiences are identified
- how communication is transformed
- how media are assigned
- how production is coordinated
- how operational consistency is preserved

This architecture therefore serves as the operational bridge between the Research Program and society.

# Design Philosophy

The architecture is built upon the following principles.

- Research First
- Projection before Production
- Transformation is not Translation
- One Research, Multiple Projections
- Separation of Responsibilities
- Preservation of Methodological Integrity

These principles enable continuous communication development without altering completed research.

# Relationship to the Research Space

The Research Space remains the canonical source of all research assets.

This architecture does not modify research.

Instead, it defines how completed Research Packages are systematically projected into society through structured operational processes.

---

# Summary

The Research-to-Society Projection Architecture establishes a reusable operational framework that connects research with society through structured projection, communication design, and production workflows.

By separating research preservation from communication design, the architecture supports sustainable long-term evolution while maintaining the structural identity of the Research Program.
