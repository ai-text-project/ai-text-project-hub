# Navigation and Projection Layer

**Architecture Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the relationship between the Navigation Layer and the Projection Layer within the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

Although both layers support communication between research and society, they perform fundamentally different functions.

The Navigation Layer preserves and organizes research.

The Projection Layer designs how research should be communicated to society.

Maintaining this separation ensures that communication evolves without altering the underlying Research Space.

---

# Two-Layer Architecture

The Research-to-Society Projection Architecture consists of two complementary operational layers.

Research Program

↓

Research Space

↓

Navigation Layer

↓

Projection Layer

↓

Society

The Navigation Layer preserves research.

The Projection Layer projects research.

---

# Navigation Layer

The Navigation Layer is implemented through the Research Space.

Its primary responsibility is to organize completed Research Packages into a coherent and navigable knowledge environment.

Typical responsibilities include:

- repository organization
- Research Package structure
- README design
- structural guides
- research asset placement
- navigation paths within the Research Space

The Navigation Layer never changes research content.

Instead, it enables users to explore completed research efficiently.

---

# Projection Layer

The Projection Layer is implemented through the Bridge-Layer Strategist.

Its responsibility begins after a Research Package has been identified within the Navigation Layer.

Projection activities include:

- audience analysis
- projection planning
- communication strategy
- phase transformation planning
- media assignment
- production coordination

The Projection Layer determines how completed research should be presented to different audiences while preserving methodological integrity.

---

# Operational Separation

Navigation and Projection operate independently.

Navigation asks:

"Where is the research?"

Projection asks:

"How should the research be communicated?"

The two layers therefore address different operational questions.

---

# Interaction between the Layers

The Projection Layer depends upon the Navigation Layer.

A Projection always begins with an existing Research Package identified within the Research Space.

The Navigation Layer provides stable research assets.

The Projection Layer generates communication pathways from those assets.

Neither layer replaces the other.

Instead, they operate cooperatively.

---

# Canonical Research Space

The Research Space functions as the canonical source of research.

All projection activities originate from this canonical source.

Communication products are therefore derived from the Research Space without modifying the original research assets.

This separation preserves long-term consistency across future communication projects.

---

# Projection Independence

Different communication products may be generated from the same Research Package.

Examples include:

- Medium articles
- enterprise presentations
- dialogue demonstrations
- educational materials
- public research guides

Each projection originates from the same Navigation Layer while following different communication pathways.

---

# Relationship to Downstream Roles

The Navigation Layer concludes once the Research Package has been identified.

The Projection Layer then coordinates downstream activities.

Typical operational flow is:

Research Space

↓

Bridge-Layer Strategist

↓

Phase Transformation Designer

↓

Bridge Content Producer

This separation enables independent development of research, transformation design, and content production.

---

# Summary

The Navigation Layer and Projection Layer together establish the operational bridge between the Research Program and society.

The Navigation Layer preserves research.

The Projection Layer projects research.

By separating these responsibilities, the architecture enables sustainable social communication while maintaining the structural identity of the Research Program.
