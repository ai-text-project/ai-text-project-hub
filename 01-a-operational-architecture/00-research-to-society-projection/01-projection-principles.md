# Projection Principles

**Architecture Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the fundamental principles governing the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

Rather than prescribing specific communication methods or media, these principles establish the shared philosophy that guides all future projection activities.

Every projection strategy, phase transformation, and communication product developed within the Bridge Layer should remain consistent with these principles.

---

# Why Projection

The objective of the Bridge Layer is not to rewrite research.

Nor is it to simplify research into popular explanations.

Instead, the Bridge Layer projects completed research into forms that are appropriate for different audiences while preserving the structural identity of the Research Program.

Projection therefore represents a change in communication, not a change in research.

---

# Core Principles

## Principle 1

### Research First

Research always remains the authoritative source.

All communication begins from completed research assets.

No projection should introduce concepts that are inconsistent with the original research.

---

## Principle 2

### Projection over Translation

Projection is fundamentally different from translation.

Translation changes language.

Projection changes communication.

The objective is to preserve research structure while adapting expression to different audiences.

---

## Principle 3

### Structural Preservation

The Bridge Layer preserves:

- conceptual relationships
- methodological consistency
- developmental continuity
- research architecture

Only the communication layer changes.

---

## Principle 4

### One Research

The Research Program produces a single evolving body of research.

Different communication products do not represent different versions of the research.

They represent different projections of the same research.

---

## Principle 5

### Multiple Projections

A single Research Package may generate multiple communication products.

Examples include:

- Medium articles
- Enterprise presentations
- Dialogue demonstrations
- Educational materials
- Public research guides
- Future communication media

Each projection serves a different communication objective while preserving the same research foundation.

---

## Principle 6

### Shared Methodological Integrity

All projections share the same methodological foundation.

Communication may differ.

Methodology does not.

The Bridge Layer therefore maintains methodological integrity across all media.

---

## Principle 7

### Audience-oriented Communication

Projection begins with audience understanding.

Different audiences require different entry points, levels of explanation, and communication styles.

Audience adaptation should never alter the underlying research.

---

## Principle 8

### Design before Production

The Bridge Layer designs communication architecture.

Content production is delegated to downstream production roles.

This separation maintains consistency across different communication media.

---

# Operational Implications

These principles influence every subsequent component of the Projection Architecture.

They guide:

- Research Package Projection Framework
- Audience Architecture
- Navigation Layer
- Phase Transformation
- Media Assignment
- Production Workflow
- Governance

All downstream operational documents should remain consistent with these principles.

---

# Summary

The Projection Principles establish a common philosophy for connecting research and society.

They ensure that communication expands accessibility without compromising the identity of the Research Program.

The Bridge Layer therefore serves not as a translation mechanism but as an architectural projection layer built upon completed research.
