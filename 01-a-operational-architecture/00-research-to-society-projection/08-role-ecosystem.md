# Role Ecosystem

**Architecture Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the operational ecosystem of roles that collectively support the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

Rather than assigning all responsibilities to a single role, the architecture separates strategic planning, communication transformation, and production into independent but cooperative functions.

This separation enables scalability, consistency, and long-term evolution of the Research Program.

---

# Fundamental Principle

The Projection Architecture is based on role specialization.

Each role has clearly defined responsibilities.

Roles collaborate through structured handover rather than overlapping responsibilities.

The objective is not organizational hierarchy, but operational coherence.

---

# Ecosystem Overview

The operational ecosystem consists of multiple complementary roles.

Research Program

↓

Research Space

↓

Bridge-Layer Strategist

↓

Phase Transformation Designer

↓

Bridge Content Producer

↓

Communication Products

Each role contributes to a different stage of the projection process.

---

# Research Space

Primary Responsibility:

Maintain the canonical Research Packages.

Typical responsibilities include:

- research asset organization
- repository management
- knowledge preservation
- navigation structure

The Research Space serves as the stable foundation for all projection activities.

---

# Bridge-Layer Strategist

Primary Responsibility:

Design how research should be projected into society.

Typical responsibilities include:

- audience architecture
- projection strategy
- communication objectives
- phase transformation strategy
- media assignment
- production workflow design

The Bridge-Layer Strategist defines the communication architecture but does not produce communication artifacts.

---

# Phase Transformation Designer

Primary Responsibility:

Design communication transformations for different audiences and communication environments.

Typical responsibilities include:

- transformation framework
- transformation matrix
- communication restructuring
- narrative adaptation
- visual transformation planning

The role preserves research integrity while redesigning communication.

---

# Bridge Content Producer

Primary Responsibility:

Produce communication artifacts according to completed projection specifications.

Typical responsibilities include:

- article production
- presentation development
- visual production
- dialogue creation
- educational content generation

Production follows the completed strategies defined upstream.

---

# Collaboration Principles

Roles collaborate through structured operational flow.

Typical sequence:

Research Space

↓

Bridge-Layer Strategist

↓

Phase Transformation Designer

↓

Bridge Content Producer

↓

Society

Each role receives completed outputs from the previous stage.

Responsibilities are transferred rather than duplicated.

---

# Boundary Preservation

Role boundaries are intentionally maintained.

Examples include:

Research Space

does not determine communication strategy.

Bridge-Layer Strategist

does not redesign research.

Phase Transformation Designer

does not redefine communication objectives.

Bridge Content Producer

does not redesign projection strategy.

This separation preserves operational consistency.

---

# Scalability

Additional roles may be introduced as the Research Program evolves.

Examples may include:

- Audience Research Specialist
- Communication Evaluation Analyst
- Campaign Coordinator
- Localization Specialist
- Outreach Coordinator

These roles extend the ecosystem without altering existing responsibilities.

---

# Evolution of the Ecosystem

The ecosystem is designed for continuous growth.

As new communication environments, audiences, or production methods emerge, additional specialized roles may be incorporated while preserving the existing operational architecture.

---

# Summary

The Role Ecosystem provides the organizational foundation of the Research-to-Society Projection Architecture.

By separating strategic planning, communication transformation, and production into complementary roles, the ecosystem enables sustainable collaboration while preserving methodological consistency throughout the Research Program.
