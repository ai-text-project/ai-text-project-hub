# Comparison Unit Definition

**Architecture Version:** v0.1 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the fundamental units of comparison used within the Comparative Observation Methodology.

Rather than treating complete research resources as indivisible objects, the methodology decomposes each resource into structural observation units.

The objective is to establish a consistent analytical basis across heterogeneous research resources.

---

---

# Fundamental Principle

The methodology does not compare complete research resources.

Instead, it compares the structural components that emerge from those resources.

Therefore, the unit of comparison is a structural observation rather than a document.

---

# Hierarchy of Comparison Units

The methodology recognizes four analytical levels.

## Level 1 — Research Resource

Examples

- Transcript
- Research Paper
- Book
- Presentation
- Repository
- Operational Document

Role

Provides observational evidence.

---

## Level 2 — Structural Component

Examples

- Scientific Discovery
- Human–AI Collaboration
- Knowledge Organization
- Structural Reorganization
- Collective Coordination

Role

Represents the primary comparison unit.

---

## Level 3 — Structural Relationship

Examples

- Correspondence
- Difference
- Complementarity
- Dependency
- Progressive Development

Role

Describes how structural components relate across multiple resources.

---

## Level 4 — Comparative Interpretation

Examples

- Comparative Framework
- Comparative Insight
- Research Skeleton

Role

Transforms structural relationships into reusable research knowledge.

---

# Relationship with the Comparative Framework

The Comparative Framework organizes multiple comparison units into stable analytical dimensions.

The Framework therefore depends upon a consistent definition of comparison units.

Without a stable comparison unit, comparative dimensions cannot remain consistent across different research resources.
