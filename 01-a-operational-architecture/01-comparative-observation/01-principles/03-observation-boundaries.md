# Observation Boundaries

**Architecture Version:** v0.1 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the scope and boundaries of the Comparative Observation Methodology.

The objective is to clarify what constitutes an appropriate subject for comparative observation and to distinguish structural observation from other forms of analysis.

These boundaries ensure methodological consistency while preventing inappropriate interpretation.

---

# Position within the Methodology

Observation Boundaries belong to the Principles layer.

```
Architecture
      │
      ▼
Principles

```

They define the methodological scope shared by all subsequent comparative observations.

---

# Fundamental Principle

Comparative Observation investigates structural organization.

It does not attempt to verify factual correctness, determine theoretical superiority, or evaluate practical effectiveness.

The methodology is concerned with how research resources organize knowledge rather than whether their conclusions are correct.

---

# Included Observation Targets

Comparative Observation may examine:

- structural organization
- reasoning processes
- knowledge organization
- Human–AI collaboration
- scientific workflows
- developmental processes
- coordination mechanisms
- interpretation structures

These elements represent the primary subjects of structural comparison.

---

# Excluded Observation Targets

The methodology does not primarily evaluate:

- factual accuracy
- scientific validity
- experimental reproducibility
- statistical significance
- implementation quality
- policy recommendations
- ethical judgment

These topics belong to other research methodologies.

---

# Independence from Domain Knowledge

Comparative Observation is designed to remain independent of specific academic disciplines.

The methodology may therefore be applied across diverse research domains provided that structural organization can be observed.

Potential application areas include:

- Artificial Intelligence
- Cognitive Science
- Psychology
- Knowledge Management
- Organizational Studies
- Educational Research

The domain changes, but the observation principles remain consistent.

---

# Independence from Individual Resources

No individual research resource is regarded as authoritative.

Each resource contributes one perspective to a broader comparative understanding.

The methodology therefore emphasizes structural integration rather than dependence upon a single source.

---

# Comparative Neutrality

Comparative Observation does not attempt to determine which resource is correct.

Instead, it seeks to identify:

- structural correspondence
- complementary perspectives
- organizational differences
- developmental relationships

Neutral observation is therefore regarded as a methodological requirement.

---

# Relationship with Structural Interpretation

Observation Boundaries determine what may legitimately be observed.

Structural Interpretation explains the meaning of those observations.

Accordingly,

```
Observation Boundaries

        │

        ▼

Structural Interpretation
```

represents the transition from methodological scope to analytical interpretation.

---

# Future Expansion

The observation boundaries may expand as the methodology develops.

Future versions may include additional observation targets while preserving the fundamental emphasis on structural organization.

Any expansion should remain consistent with the methodological principles defined within this architecture.
