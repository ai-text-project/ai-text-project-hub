# Correspondence Analysis

**Architecture Version:** v0.1 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the process of Correspondence Analysis within the Comparative Observation Methodology.

The objective is to examine the structural correspondence identified during Structural Interpretation and to clarify how these relationships contribute to comparative understanding.

Correspondence Analysis extends interpretation by organizing multiple structural observations into coherent analytical relationships.

# Analysis Objective

The objective of Correspondence Analysis is to examine how structural correspondence is distributed across multiple research resources.

Rather than evaluating individual observations independently, the analysis seeks recurring organizational relationships that contribute to comparative understanding.

---

# Input

Correspondence Analysis uses:

- Structural Interpretation
- Comparative Records
- Structural Correspondence
- Comparison Units

All analytical conclusions should remain traceable to documented observations.

---

---

# Analytical Perspectives

Correspondence Analysis may examine:

- recurring organizational relationships;
- complementary functional structures;
- shared developmental patterns;
- alternative structural configurations;
- consistent coordination mechanisms.

The analysis focuses on relationships rather than isolated observations.

---

# Multiple Correspondence

A single research resource may participate in multiple structural correspondence relationships.

Likewise, a single correspondence may emerge across several independent resources.

Accordingly, correspondence should be regarded as a network of structural relationships rather than a one-to-one comparison.

---

# Analytical Principles

Correspondence Analysis follows several methodological principles.

- Preserve traceability.
- Analyze relationships rather than isolated observations.
- Consider multiple possible organizational explanations.
- Avoid premature theoretical conclusions.
- Maintain consistency with the Comparative Framework.
