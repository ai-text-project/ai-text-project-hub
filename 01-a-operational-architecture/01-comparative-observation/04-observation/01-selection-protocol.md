# Selection Protocol

**Architecture Version:** v0.1 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the standard protocol for selecting research resources from the Comparative Observation Corpus.

The objective is to construct an observation set that is structurally informative while maintaining methodological consistency and analytical diversity.

Selection is regarded as an operational process that prepares research resources for comparative observation.

# Selection Objective

The objective of selection is not to collect as many resources as possible.

Instead, the objective is to construct an observation set capable of revealing meaningful structural correspondence.

Selection therefore emphasizes analytical value rather than quantity.

---

# Selection Criteria

Research resources may be selected according to multiple complementary criteria.

## Structural Relevance

Resources should contain observable structural organizations related to the current comparative objective.

Examples include:

- reasoning processes
- organizational structures
- knowledge formation
- Human–AI collaboration
- scientific workflows

---

## Comparative Diversity

The observation set should include resources representing different perspectives.

Diversity may include:

- academic disciplines
- theoretical approaches
- communication media
- organizational viewpoints

Diversity increases the opportunity to discover structural correspondence.

---

## Independence

Selected resources should represent independently developed viewpoints whenever possible.

Independence reduces the likelihood that structural similarities result solely from direct influence.

---

## Observational Value

Priority should be given to resources expected to contribute meaningful structural observations.

Selection therefore focuses on observational potential rather than publication status or popularity.

---

# Selection Process

The standard selection process consists of five operational steps.

```
Corpus

      │

      ▼

Define Observation Objective

      │

      ▼

Identify Candidate Resources

      │

      ▼

Evaluate Structural Relevance

      │

      ▼

Construct Observation Set
```

The resulting Observation Set serves as the input for Comparative Observation.

---

# Selection Principles

Selection should satisfy the following principles.

- Maintain structural diversity.
- Avoid unnecessary duplication.
- Prefer complementary perspectives.
- Preserve traceability.
- Keep the observation objective explicit.

---

# Selection Is Context-Dependent

The same research resource may be selected for different comparative studies.

Selection therefore depends upon the observation objective rather than the intrinsic characteristics of an individual resource.

Consequently, no research resource is permanently designated as either "important" or "unimportant."

Its observational value depends upon the context of the comparative study.

---

# Relationship with Comparative Observation

Selection prepares an observation set.

Comparative Observation analyzes the selected resources.

Accordingly,

```
Selection

        │

        ▼

Comparative Observation
```

maintains a clear separation between resource preparation and structural observation.

---

# Future Development

Future versions may introduce:

- priority scoring;
- corpus recommendation support;
- automated candidate discovery;
- observation history feedback.

Such enhancements should assist selection without replacing methodological judgment.
