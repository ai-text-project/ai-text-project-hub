# Comparative Record Template

**Architecture Version:** v0.1 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the standard template for recording the results of comparative observation.

The objective is to preserve structural observations in a consistent and traceable format before interpretation begins.

The Comparative Record serves as the primary observational evidence for subsequent structural interpretation and research development.

---

# Position within the Methodology

The Comparative Record belongs to the Observation layer.


The Comparative Record concludes the Observation layer.

---

# Recording Principles

A Comparative Record should:

- preserve observational neutrality;
- distinguish observation from interpretation;
- maintain traceability;
- support future comparative studies; and
- remain reusable as research evidence.

The record should describe what was observed rather than explain why it occurred.

---

# Standard Record Structure

## Observation ID

Unique identifier for the comparative observation.

Example

```
CO-001
```

---

## Observation Objective

Describe the purpose of the current comparative observation.

Example

```
To compare the organizational structures underlying scientific reasoning across multiple research resources.
```

---

## Compared Resources

List the research resources included in the observation.

Example

- Resource A
- Resource B
- Resource C

---

## Comparison Units

Specify the comparison units defined by the Comparative Framework.

Examples

- reasoning process
- knowledge organization
- collaboration structure
- developmental sequence

---

## Observed Structural Correspondence

Describe structural relationships identified during comparative observation.

Examples

- recurring organizational patterns;
- common developmental structures;
- complementary functional roles;
- shared coordination mechanisms.

Only directly observable correspondence should be recorded.

---

## Observed Structural Differences

Describe meaningful structural differences identified across the resources.

Differences should be documented objectively without evaluating superiority.

---

## Observation Notes

Record additional observations that may assist later interpretation.

Examples include:

- unexpected structural patterns;
- possible future comparison targets;
- methodological observations.

These notes remain observational rather than interpretative.

---

# Traceability

Each Comparative Record should maintain links to:

- source resources;
- observation date;
- observation framework;
- related comparative records.

Traceability supports reproducibility and long-term methodological consistency.

---

# Relationship with Interpretation

The Comparative Record represents the endpoint of observation.

Interpretation begins after observational evidence has been documented.

Accordingly,

```
Comparative Record

        │

        ▼

Structural Interpretation
```

preserves a clear methodological boundary.

---

# Future Development

Future versions may incorporate:

- structured observation databases;
- comparative visualization support;
- automated observation indexing;
- cross-reference management.

These enhancements should preserve the observational integrity of each record.
