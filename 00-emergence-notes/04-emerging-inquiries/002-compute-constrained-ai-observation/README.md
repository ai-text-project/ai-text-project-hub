# Purpose

This directory preserves an emerging inquiry concerning the relationship among:

```text
Compute Scale
      ×
Process Architecture
      ×
Observation Capability
      ×
Externalized Research State
```

The inquiry asks whether some forms of high-complexity AI research activity can be supported under computational constraints through the organization of the research process itself.

The central interest is not whether a computationally constrained environment can reproduce the raw computational capacity, throughput, parallelism, or autonomy of a large-scale AI research infrastructure.

Instead, the inquiry examines whether operations such as:

```text
Structure-Preserving Decomposition
        ↓
Bounded Observation
        ↓
Judgment
        ↓
Freeze
        ↓
Externalization
        ↓
Reintegration
```

may allow complex research objects to remain observable and operationally manageable across limited AI contexts and successive research stages.

---

# Current Research Question

The current research question is:

> **To what extent can Process Architecture support advanced AI observation under computational constraints?**

A more operational formulation is:

> Can a complex research object be decomposed into bounded Observation Objects while preserving the structure required for local observation, judgment, externalization, and later reintegration?

This question remains open.

---

# Current Working Hypothesis

The inquiry currently contains one Working Hypothesis:

> **Advanced AI research capability may not be determined solely by model scale or computational resources.**
>
> **Structure-Preserving Decomposition, Observation, Judgment, Freeze, Externalization, and Reintegration may allow a computationally constrained LLM environment to perform some high-complexity research processes that would otherwise exceed the practical working context of a single interaction or instance.**

The hypothesis remains:

> **Working Hypothesis / Unverified**

It has not been experimentally validated.

# Observation Trigger

The immediate trigger for this inquiry was an external case describing a large-scale AI-assisted verification process applied to a highly complex mathematical object.

The mathematical subject itself is not the primary research object of this directory.

The relevant trigger was the reported process architecture:

- decomposition of a large verification target;
- distributed or parallel AI work;
- intermediate checking;
- failure detection;
- replanning;
- repeated processing;
- formal verification;
- reintegration.

This raised a broader question:

> Is some portion of advanced AI research capability attributable not only to computational scale, but also to the architecture through which complex work is decomposed, observed, checked, preserved, and reintegrated?

The external case is therefore retained conceptually as an:

> **Observation Trigger**

and not as validation evidence.

---

# Core Structural Distinction

A central distinction currently governing this inquiry is:

```text
Simple Decomposition
        ≠
Structure-Preserving Decomposition
```

Reducing the size of an active context is not sufficient if the decomposition destroys the relations required to understand or reconstruct the larger research object.

Potential preservation requirements include:

```text
Object Boundary
Dependency
Responsibility
Relation
Evidence Provenance
Sequence
Structural Status
Integration Path
```

The inquiry therefore concerns not merely how to make a research object smaller, but:

> **how to make it locally observable without destroying the structure required for later reintegration.**

# Evidence Status

The present inquiry distinguishes among three levels.

## Observed

Existing research practice has repeatedly used bounded intake, observation, structural projection, Freeze, externalized artifacts, comparison, and later reintegration across complex research tasks.

## Structurally Inferred

These operations may reduce the amount of active context required at any single research stage while preserving a larger research process across time and artifacts.

## Hypothetical

It remains unverified whether such Process Architecture can compensate for computational constraints to a meaningful degree.

It also remains unknown under what conditions local observations can be reintegrated without loss or distortion of global structure.
