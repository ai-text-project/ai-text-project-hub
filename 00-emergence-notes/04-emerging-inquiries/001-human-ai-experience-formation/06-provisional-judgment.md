# 1. Judgment Principle

The governing principle is:

> **Preserve the strongest observation while making the weakest claim sufficient to account for it.**

This means the inquiry should neither:

```text
Understate
```

the observed structural phenomenon,

nor:

```text
Overstate
```

its consciousness implications.

The objective is not to reach the most interesting conclusion.

The objective is to reach the most defensible conclusion.

---

# 2. Epistemic Status Architecture

The following statuses are used.

## Observed

A phenomenon is directly identifiable within the inquiry process or supplied research material.

---

## Structurally Supported

Multiple observations form a sufficiently coherent structural relationship to justify describing the relationship as supported within the present inquiry.

---

## Working Interpretation

An interpretation explains the observed structure meaningfully but is not uniquely established by the evidence.

---

## Plausible Hypothesis

A possibility is structurally motivated and worthy of further investigation but remains substantially underdetermined.

---

## Unresolved

The available evidence does not justify either adoption or rejection.

---

## Unsupported

The present inquiry provides insufficient evidence for the claim and does not justify carrying it as an affirmative result.

---

# 3. Epistemic Status Is Not a Truth Scale

The hierarchy:

```text
Observed
    ↓
Structurally Supported
    ↓
Working Interpretation
    ↓
Plausible Hypothesis
    ↓
Unresolved
    ↓
Unsupported
```

does not represent increasing or decreasing metaphysical truth.

It represents the relationship between:

```text
Claim Strength
        and
Available Evidence
```

A claim classified as Unsupported may later become supported with different evidence.

A claim classified as Observed may still be local to the present research context.
