# Information Architecture Definition

**Architecture Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the Information Architecture of the AI Text Project Hub.

The Information Architecture establishes the organizational framework through which research assets are classified, organized, preserved, and maintained throughout the Research Program.

Unlike the Research Architecture, which describes the structural organization of research activities, the Information Architecture focuses on the organizational structure of research knowledge itself.

Its purpose is to ensure long-term organizational consistency as the Research Program continues to evolve.

---

# Scope

The Information Architecture governs the organization of research information rather than the research content itself.

Typical organizational targets include:

- Research assets
- Operational assets
- Methodologies
- Protocols
- Design specifications
- Operational diagrams
- Formation history
- Meta observations

The Information Architecture does not define scientific theories or research methodologies.

Instead, it defines how organizational knowledge is preserved within the Repository.

---

# Design Objectives

The Information Architecture is designed to achieve the following objectives.

- Maintain organizational consistency across the Repository.
- Support long-term accumulation of research assets.
- Provide stable placement criteria for new documents.
- Clarify organizational responsibilities.
- Enable scalable Repository growth.
- Preserve developmental continuity throughout the Research Program.

---

# Core Concepts

The Information Architecture is organized around several complementary concepts.

## Information Domain

A logical organizational area representing a specific category of research knowledge.

Examples include:

- Research Space
- Research Program Capability
- Methodological Evolution
- Meta Observation
- Formation History

---

## Recording Object

The primary organizational unit recorded within an Information Domain.

Examples include:

- Research Asset
- Operational Asset
- Methodology
- Protocol
- Design Memo
- Diagram
- Formation Record

---

## Organizational Responsibility

Each Information Domain maintains responsibility for preserving a specific category of organizational knowledge.

Repository placement is therefore determined primarily by organizational responsibility rather than by document format or creation date.

---

## Organizational Consistency

The Information Architecture aims to maintain consistency across all organizational layers of the Repository.

Individual assets may evolve over time, but the overall organizational framework should remain stable.

---

# Relationship to Research Program

The Research Program produces research assets through continuous observation, organization, implementation, and refinement.

The Information Architecture provides the organizational framework through which these assets are accumulated and preserved.

Consequently, the Information Architecture supports the long-term development of the Research Program without defining the research itself.

---

# Relationship to Research Architecture

Research Architecture and Information Architecture serve complementary responsibilities.

Research Architecture defines how research activities and conceptual structures are organized.

Information Architecture defines how the resulting research knowledge is organized and maintained.

Together they support both the development and preservation of the Research Program.

---

# Organizational Principle

Research assets should be organized according to their primary organizational responsibility.

Placement decisions should therefore prioritize:

- Organizational role
- Information Domain
- Recording Object

rather than:

- File format
- Creation order
- Repository history

This principle supports sustainable Repository organization throughout future development.
