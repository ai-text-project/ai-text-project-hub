# Information Domains

**Architecture Version:** v1.0 (Provisional)

---

# Purpose

This document defines the Information Domains used throughout the Information Architecture of the AI Text Project Hub.

An Information Domain represents a major organizational responsibility for preserving a specific category of research knowledge.

Information Domains provide the primary organizational framework through which research assets are classified, organized, and maintained.

---

# Definition

An Information Domain is a logical organizational area responsible for preserving a particular class of organizational knowledge.

Unlike individual folders, which contain documents, an Information Domain defines:

- what type of knowledge is recorded,
- why it is recorded,
- and how it contributes to the long-term development of the Research Program.

Each research asset should therefore belong primarily to one Information Domain.

# Current Information Domains

# Organizational Relationship

The Information Domains collectively represent complementary organizational responsibilities.

Current observations suggest the following developmental relationship.

Research Space

↓

Research Program Capability

↓

Methodological Evolution

↓

Meta Observation

↓

Formation History

This progression reflects the current organizational interpretation and remains subject to future refinement.

---

# Information Domain Assignment

Research assets should be assigned to the Information Domain that best represents their primary organizational responsibility.

Assignment should prioritize:

- organizational purpose,
- recorded knowledge,
- and long-term organizational role,

rather than:

- file type,
- document format,
- or creation date.

---

# Relationship to Folder Structure

Folders represent the physical implementation of Information Domains within the Repository.

An Information Domain is therefore a conceptual organizational layer.

Folders are one possible implementation of that conceptual layer.

Consequently, Information Domains remain conceptually stable even if future folder structures evolve.
